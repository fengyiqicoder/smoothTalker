# Declining an invitation

## Goal
The host should finish reading and think "fair enough, they still like me", not "was it something I did?"

## Structure
1. **Thank for the specific invite** (one clause).
2. **Clear no** in the first two lines. Do not bury it.
3. **One reason, short**, or none. A true simple reason beats a detailed excuse. Detailed excuses invite negotiation.
4. **Signal the relationship** with a concrete alternative or a specific warm line.
5. **Stop.** No second apology.

## Principles that matter here
- The longer the excuse, the more it sounds like a lie.
- "Maybe" is crueller than "no" because it makes them plan around you.
- If you would go if it were a different day, say so and propose one. If you would not go regardless, do not propose anything; just be warm.

## Examples

**Friend's party, text:**
> Ah, I can't do Saturday, I've already got plans I can't move. Gutted to miss it. Let's do a proper catch-up the week after? I'll cook.

**Wedding, message to a not-close friend:**
> Thank you so much for inviting us, it means a lot. Unfortunately we won't be able to make it on the 14th. We'll be thinking of you both and would love to take you out to celebrate once you're back.

**Recurring thing you want to stop going to, no reason:**
> I'm going to sit this one out. Have a great time and send me photos.

**Work social:**
> I'll pass on Thursday drinks this time, but enjoy. Count me in for the next one.

## Avoid
- "I'll try to make it" when you know you will not.
- Listing your whole week.
- "Sorry sorry sorry".
- Cancelling on the day when you knew a week ago.

## If they push
> I really can't this time. Next one, I promise.
Repeat once. Do not add new reasons; new reasons are new things to argue with.
