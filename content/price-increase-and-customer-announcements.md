# Announcing a price increase, delay or change to customers

## Goal
Customers feel informed and respected, not blindsided or spun. Most people accept a change; they do not accept feeling tricked.

## Structure
1. **The change, in the first sentence.** Date and amount.
2. **The reason, one honest line.** Costs, wages, quality. Not a paragraph.
3. **What isn't changing** (quality, service, the thing they love).
4. **What they can do**: lock in the old price, cancel easily, get help.
5. **Thanks, and a real name.**

## Principles
- Early. Give real notice (30 days is standard; more for big changes).
- Plain. "Prices are going up 8% from 1 March" beats "we're adjusting our pricing structure".
- No apology for a fair increase. "Sorry" makes people think you're doing something wrong.
- Make the exit easy. Customers who can leave easily are more likely to stay.
- Send it from a person, not "The Team".
- Do not over-justify. Every extra reason invites debate.

## Price increase

**Small business, email to customers:**
> Hi Sam,
> From 1 March, our prices are going up by about 8%: the medium candle moves from $28 to $30, and the large from $42 to $45. This is the first increase in three years and it covers what wax and shipping now cost us. Nothing about how we make them changes. If you'd like to stock up at current prices, the old prices hold on the site until the end of February. Thank you for keeping a small business going.
> Ana

**Freelancer, to an existing client:**
> Hi Rob,
> I'm updating my rates for the new year. From 1 January, my day rate moves from $600 to $680. For you, I'll hold the current rate through March so it doesn't affect the project in flight. I'm grateful for the work we've done together and I'm looking forward to more of it.
> Nina

**Subscription service:**
> Your plan is changing from $9 to $11 a month from your next billing date after 1 April. Since you've been with us a while, you'll stay at $9 until July. You can cancel any time from your account page, no hoops. Here's what the extra covers: [two lines].

## Delay

> Hi Sam, your order #4521 is going to arrive later than promised: the new estimate is 22 May, about a week late. A supplier issue on our side, not yours. If that doesn't work for you, reply and I'll refund it in full, no questions. Otherwise, I'll send tracking the moment it ships. Sorry for the wait.
> Ana

**Bigger delay, many customers:**
> We've hit a manufacturing delay and orders placed since 1 May will ship around 10 June instead of the end of May. If you'd rather not wait, reply to this email for an immediate full refund. If you can wait, we'll add [small make-good] to your order. We'll email again the day it ships. Thank you for your patience, and I'm sorry.

## Closure or ending a service

> After six years, we're closing the shop on 30 September. It's a personal decision, and a good one for us, but we'll miss this. Between now and then: everything's 20% off, gift cards can be used until the 30th (email us if you can't make it), and we'll help you find a new supplier for anything you regularly order. Thank you for six extraordinary years.

## Policy change (returns, hours, minimums)

> From 1 June, our return window changes from 60 days to 30. Anything ordered before 1 June keeps the 60-day window. The reason: returns after a month were mostly worn items we can't resell, and it was costing more than we could absorb. Everything else about returns stays the same: free label, fast refund.

## Avoid
- "Due to unforeseen circumstances."
- "We regret to inform you."
- Hiding the number.
- Sending it from "no-reply".
- Blaming customers or "some people".
- Burying the change in a newsletter.
