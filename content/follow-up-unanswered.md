# Following up

## Goal
Make it easy for them to reply now, without making them feel bad for not replying earlier. Silence usually means busy or forgot, not rejection.

## Structure
1. **Assume the best out loud** ("I know things are hectic").
2. **Restate the thing in one line** so they do not have to scroll.
3. **Make the reply tiny**: a yes/no, a pick between two options, or "even a 'not now' is helpful".
4. **Optional deadline** framed as helping you plan, not pressuring them.

## Escalation ladder

**Bump 1 (after 3 to 5 days), lightest touch:**
> Bumping this in case it got buried. Thursday or Friday for the call?

**Bump 2 (after another week), give a reason and an out:**
> Hey, checking in on the draft I sent. If it's not a priority right now that's completely fine, I'd just like to know so I can plan around it.

**Bump 3 (final), close the loop cleanly:**
> Last nudge from me on this. I'll assume it's a no for now and stop pestering, but the door's open if it becomes useful later.

Three is the maximum. After that, silence is the answer.

## Examples

**Friend who went quiet, personal:**
> Hey, no agenda, just realised it's been a while. How are you doing?

**After sending someone something they asked for:**
> Did the file come through okay? Let me know if you need a different format.

**Someone owes you an answer on plans:**
> Need to lock the booking by tomorrow. Are you in or out? Either is fine, I just need to know.

**Email, professional:**
> Hi Marcus,
> Following up on the proposal from the 12th. Happy to adjust scope or timing if that would help. If it's easier, a one-line "not now" is genuinely useful.
> Thanks,
> Jo

## Avoid
- "Friendly reminder" (never friendly).
- "Just checking if you saw my message?" (they did).
- "?" or "??" alone.
- Restating the entire original message.
- Following up with the same message twice.
- Guilt: "I guess you're too busy for me."

## Reading the silence
If it is a friend and this happens repeatedly, the follow-up should eventually be about the pattern, not the message. See `reconnect-after-silence` and `set-boundary`.
