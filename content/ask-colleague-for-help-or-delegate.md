# Asking a colleague for help, delegating, saying "I don't know"

## Asking a peer for help

**Structure:** what you've tried → the specific thing you need → the size of the ask → an out.

> Hey Lee, I've been stuck on the export bug for an hour and I've checked the obvious things (config, permissions). Could you look at it with me for 15 minutes today? If you're heads-down, tomorrow's fine.

> Could I borrow your eyes on this deck before it goes to Sara? Mainly slides 4 to 7, where I'm not sure the story lands. Ten minutes max.

**Principles**
- Show your work first. "I've tried X and Y" turns "help me" into "help me finish".
- Name the time cost. People say yes to 15 minutes and dodge "a quick chat".
- Give a deadline and an out.
- Say thanks afterwards, specifically, and ideally where their manager can see it.

## Delegating (to a peer, a junior, or across teams)

**Structure:** the outcome → why them → what "done" looks like → deadline → what support you'll give → check they can take it.

> Priya, I'd like you to own the onboarding email sequence for the launch. You've done the best work on the welcome flows, so it's a natural fit. Done means: five emails drafted and reviewed by marketing by the 20th. I'll do a first pass on your drafts whenever you want, and I'll clear it with Dan so it's on your plate officially. Can you take it on, given what you've got already?

**Peer, no authority:**
> Could you take the vendor calls this week? I'm on the audit until Thursday and you've got the relationship there. I'll pick up your Friday standup in return.

**Cross-team:**
> Hi Marcus, we need the API docs updated for the release and I think your team's the right owner since you built the endpoints. Target is the 15th. Is that doable, or should we talk about what would make it possible?

**Principles**
- Describe the outcome, not the steps. People own outcomes; they resent step lists.
- Say why them. It's not flattery, it's context.
- Ask, and mean it. If they say they can't, negotiate scope or date.
- Delegating and then micromanaging is worse than not delegating.
- Take responsibility upward. If it fails, that's yours.

## Saying "I don't know"

**The formula:** I don't know + what I'll do about it + when.

> I don't know off the top of my head. Let me check and I'll have an answer by 3.
> Good question, I'm not sure. My guess is X but I don't want to give you a guess on this. I'll confirm today.
> That's outside what I've worked on. Lee has; want me to loop them in?
> I don't know, and I'd rather say that than make something up. What's the decision this feeds into? That'll help me get you the right thing.

**Principles**
- "I don't know" said fast and cleanly raises your credibility. Bluffing lowers it, later.
- Always attach the next step.
- In meetings, "I'll find out" beats a wrong answer every time.

## Avoid
- "Sorry to bother you, I know you're busy, but..."
- Asking for help without saying what you've tried.
- Delegating by dumping ("can you just handle this?").
- Delegating in a group message so nobody owns it.
- "I think it's probably..." when you don't know.
- Apologising for not knowing.
