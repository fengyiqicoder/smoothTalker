# Telling someone something awkward about themselves

## Goal
They find out privately, fix it, and are grateful rather than humiliated. The rule of thumb: if they can fix it in the next five minutes (food in teeth, an open fly, a typo), always tell them. If it is ongoing and personal (body odour, breath), tell them once, privately, and only if you are the right person.

For feedback on someone's work or behaviour, use `give-feedback-kindly` or `give-feedback-to-colleague`.

## Structure
1. **Private channel, right away.** A DM, a quiet word, a discreet gesture. Never in front of others or in a public reply.
2. **Say it plainly in one sentence.** Euphemisms prolong the discomfort.
3. **Normalise it.** One short line that it happens to everyone.
4. **Give them an exit**, and move on. Do not linger, do not bring it up again.

## Principles
- Speed is kindness for quick fixes. Five seconds of awkwardness saves them an afternoon of embarrassment.
- The more personal the issue, the more private and the more considered the message. Body odour and breath deserve a face-to-face word or a private message, not a note left on the desk.
- Who says it matters. A close friend, a manager, or a trusted peer is better than a near-stranger. At work, persistent hygiene issues are usually for the manager, handled privately and with care.
- Assume a reason you do not know about: a medical condition, a broken washing machine, a new medication, a hard stretch at home. Leave room for it without asking.
- Say it once. If they fix it, it never happened.
- Do not gather a group opinion first. "We all noticed" turns a kindness into a verdict.

## Examples

**Food in teeth or something on their face (quiet, in person):**
> Quick one, you've got a bit of something in your teeth, left side.

**Open fly or wardrobe issue, before a meeting:**
> Heads up before you go in, your fly's down. Saved you!

**Typo in a public post (DM, not a comment):**
> Loved the post! Tiny thing, the second paragraph says "pubic sector" instead of "public". Thought you'd want to catch it before more people see it.

**Wrong date on an event invite they sent out:**
> Hey, just checking: the invite says Saturday the 14th, but the 14th is a Friday. Which one did you mean? Might be worth a quick correction.

**Close friend, breath (in person or private text):**
> This is a bit awkward, and I'm only saying it because I'd want you to tell me: I've noticed your breath has been a bit off lately. Could be nothing, but a dentist might know. Anyway, that's it, carry on!

**Colleague you are close to, body odour (private, in person):**
> Can I mention something a bit personal, just between us? I've noticed a bit of a smell sometimes in the afternoons. I'd want someone to tell me, so I'm telling you. No big deal, and I won't bring it up again.

**Manager to a report, persistent hygiene issue (private meeting):**
> I want to raise something a bit sensitive, and it stays between us. A few times recently there's been a noticeable body odour. I'm not assuming anything about why, and if there's something going on that we can help with, like a health issue or something at home, I'd like to know. Can we agree you'll keep an eye on it?

## Avoid
- Telling them in front of others, or replying publicly to their post.
- Anonymous notes, gifts of deodorant, or "hints".
- "Everyone's been talking about it."
- Long preambles that make them fear something far worse.
- Laughing, or telling the story later.
- Asking why it is happening.

## If it goes badly
If they are embarrassed, lighten it and move on: say it happens to everyone and change the subject. If they get defensive or hurt, do not repeat the details or add evidence; say you only mentioned it because you would want to know, and drop it. If a work hygiene issue continues after one private conversation, it becomes a manager's issue to raise privately again, with an offer of support, not a peer's to keep repeating.
> Honestly, it happens to everyone, I've done it twice this week. Right, coffee?
> I didn't mean to upset you. I only said it because I'd want someone to tell me. That's all, I won't mention it again.
