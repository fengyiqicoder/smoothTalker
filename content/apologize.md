# Apologising

## Goal
The other person feels that you understand what you did, that you own it, and that it is less likely to happen again. Forgiveness is their choice, not the message's goal.

## Structure (all four, in order)
1. **Name the specific thing.** "I'm sorry I missed your call and didn't text back until today." Not "sorry about earlier."
2. **Own it without a "but".** No "but I was busy". If there is context they need, it goes after ownership, in one clause, framed as explanation not excuse.
3. **Acknowledge the impact on them.** "That left you waiting and probably wondering if I'd flaked."
4. **What changes.** A concrete fix, or a concrete future behaviour. Or simply "It won't happen again" if that is true.

Optional: **one make-good** if proportionate.

## Principles
- Scale the apology to the offence. Over-apologising for small things makes the other person manage your guilt.
- "I'm sorry you feel that way" is not an apology. "I'm sorry if" is not an apology.
- Do not ask for forgiveness in the same message. It turns the apology into a request.
- Say it once. Repetition makes it about your discomfort.

## Examples

**Late to meet a friend, text:**
> Sorry I kept you waiting 25 minutes, I should have texted the moment I knew I was running behind. Drinks are on me tonight.

**Forgot something important:**
> I completely dropped the ball on your birthday and I'm sorry. No excuse, I just didn't put it in my calendar and it slipped. That's not the kind of friend I want to be. Can I take you out this weekend, properly?

**Hurt someone with what you said:**
> I've been thinking about what I said on Sunday about your job. It was unfair and it came out as contempt, which isn't what I feel. I can see it stung. I'm sorry. I'd like to hear how it landed for you, when you're up for it.

**Missed a deadline, work:**
> I missed the Friday deadline on the report and I'm sorry, that put you in a bad spot with the client. It's done now and attached. Going forward I'll flag on Wednesday if anything is at risk so you're never surprised.

**Small thing, keep it small:**
> Oops, my bad, sending now.

## Avoid
- "I'm sorry, but..."
- "I'm sorry you feel..."
- "Mistakes were made."
- Explaining at length. The longer the explanation, the more it reads as self-defence.
- Asking "are we okay?" in the same message. Give them room.

## If they are still upset
> That's fair. I'm not going to argue with how you feel about it. I'm here when you're ready.
Then actually leave space.
