# Co-parenting messages with an ex

## Goal
The arrangement for the children works, and every message could be read by the children, a mediator or a judge without embarrassing you. Co-parenting messages are a business correspondence about the most important project you share. Warmth is optional; clarity and civility are not.

The relationship itself (getting back together, closure, belongings): `respond-to-an-ex-or-breakup-message`.

## Structure
1. **Brief greeting.** Name, no small talk required.
2. **One topic per message.** The swap, the cost, the school trip.
3. **The facts and the specific proposal**, with dates, times and amounts.
4. **A clear question they can answer yes or no**, with the date you need it by.
5. **Sign off neutrally.** "Thanks" is enough.

## Principles
- Brief, informative, friendly, firm. If a sentence is not needed to arrange the children's lives, cut it.
- Keep the past out. No "as usual", "once again", "you always". Each message is about this weekend, not the marriage.
- Put the children's needs first and name them concretely ("Mia has her recital at 5") rather than as leverage ("the kids are upset because of you").
- Never send messages through the children, and never ask them to report back.
- Write it, do not phone it, for anything that matters: changes, money, medical decisions. A shared calendar or co-parenting app makes a record without you having to keep one.
- You do not have to reply to hostility, only to logistics. Answer the question in the message and ignore the rest.
- Give reasonable notice and grant reasonable swaps. Flexibility you give is flexibility you are more likely to get.

## Examples

**Swap request:**
> Hi Sam, could we swap weekends this month? I'd take the kids 14 to 16 and you'd have 21 to 23, because of my sister's wedding. Can you let me know by Wednesday?

**Late handover, first time it becomes a pattern:**
> Hi Alex, the last three handovers have been 30 to 45 minutes after 6. Leo gets unsettled waiting. Can we either stick to 6 or agree 6:30 as the new time? Either works for me as long as it's consistent.

**Shared cost:**
> Hi, the school trip to the science museum is £60, due by 10 March. Receipt attached. As agreed, could you send £30 by then? Thanks.

**Medical or school update, just the facts:**
> Mia saw the GP today for her ear. It's a mild infection, she's on antibiotics twice a day until Sunday. The bottle and instructions are in her bag. She's fine for school tomorrow.

**New partner meeting the children:**
> I wanted to let you know before it happens: I've been seeing someone for about six months, and I'm planning for them to meet the kids briefly at the park next month. I'll keep it low-key. If you have any concerns, I'm happy to talk them through.

**Replying to a long angry message (answer only the logistics):**
> Noted about Saturday. I'll have the kids ready at 10 as planned.

**Setting a communication channel:**
> To keep things simple, could we move all scheduling to the co-parenting app from now on? Then we both have the calendar and the costs in one place.

## Avoid
- Sarcasm, "per my last message", or anything you would not want read aloud in court.
- Commenting on their parenting, new partner or lifestyle, unless it is a genuine safety issue.
- Using the children as messengers or as arguments.
- Replying at night or while angry. Draft it, sleep, send the shorter version in the morning.
- Long messages mixing three topics. The hardest topic swallows the easy ones.
- Withholding contact or information as leverage over money.

## If it goes badly
If they respond with insults or history, do not match them. Reply only to the practical part, or wait until there is one. If they refuse a reasonable swap, accept it and plan around it; one refusal is not a pattern. If a pattern of lateness, missed payments or cancelled contact continues, keep a dated record and raise it through mediation or a family lawyer rather than escalating by text. If you ever believe a child is unsafe, contact the relevant authorities or emergency services directly; that is not a messaging problem.
> I'm not going to go back over that. For Saturday: 10am at mine, as planned.
> Understood, I'll keep this weekend as it is. Let me know if a different date works later in the month.
