# Chasing a late payment

## Goal
You get paid, the relationship survives, and you sound like a business, not a friend asking for a favour.

## Principles
- Assume admin error until proven otherwise. Most late payments are someone forgetting to click.
- Every message has: invoice number, amount, original due date, how to pay. They should be able to pay from the message.
- Firmness increases with each message; warmth stays constant until the final notice.
- Never apologise for chasing. Never "sorry to bother you."
- Cc accounts payable or the finance contact from message two onward.
- Put a date on everything.

## The escalation

**Stage 1: due date passed, 3 to 5 days (gentle):**
> Hi Sarah,
> A quick note that invoice #1042 for $2,400 was due on the 10th and I haven't seen it come through yet. It may just be in the queue; if so, ignore this. Payment details are on the invoice, attached again for convenience.
> Thanks,
> Mia

**Stage 2: two weeks late (direct):**
> Hi Sarah,
> Following up on invoice #1042 ($2,400, due 10 May), now two weeks overdue. Could you let me know when I can expect payment, or if there's an issue on your end I should know about? I've copied in accounts in case that helps.
> Thanks,
> Mia

**Stage 3: 30 days late (firm, with consequence):**
> Hi Sarah,
> Invoice #1042 ($2,400) is now 30 days overdue. I need to receive payment by Friday 14 June. If it's not received by then, I'll have to pause work on the current phase and apply the late fee set out in our agreement. I'd much rather not do either, so please let me know today if there's a problem I can help resolve.
> Regards,
> Mia

**Final notice (before formal action):**
> This is a final notice for invoice #1042 ($2,400), 45 days overdue. If payment isn't received by [date], I'll refer the matter to [collections / small claims]. I'm still happy to resolve this directly; a call today would be enough.

## Variations

**Client says "it's being processed":**
> Thanks. Could you confirm the payment date so I can plan around it?

**Client disputes the amount:**
> Happy to go through it. Which line are you querying? The rest of the invoice isn't in dispute, so could that portion be paid now while we sort the remainder?

**Friend or informal client:**
> Hey, the $600 for the design work was due last week. Can you sort it this week? Bank details are in the invoice.

## Avoid
- "Just checking in!"
- "I know things are busy but..."
- Emotional language ("I'm really struggling because you haven't paid").
- Threats you will not follow through on.
- Continuing to deliver new work while heavily unpaid.
- Chasing in the same thread as friendly chat. Separate email, clear subject line: "Overdue: Invoice #1042".
