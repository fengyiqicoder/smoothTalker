# De-escalating an argument

## Goal
Get both of you out of fight mode. Not to resolve the issue, not to win, not to surrender. Resolution comes later, calmer.

## Structure
1. **Name what's happening, not who's wrong.** "We're both heated."
2. **Take responsibility for your part, specifically**, even if it's small. "I got sharp with you."
3. **Reflect their feeling accurately**, without agreeing with their argument. "You feel like I wasn't listening."
4. **Propose a pause with a return time.** "Can we pick this up after dinner?" A pause without a return time feels like abandonment.
5. **One line of the relationship being bigger than this.**

## Principles
- Nobody has ever been calmed by "calm down."
- Being right is not available right now. Take it off the table.
- Do not litigate facts mid-fight. "That's not what I said" restarts it.
- Say less. Every sentence in an argument is ammunition.
- If you need to leave, say when you'll be back. "I need 20 minutes and then I'm coming back to this."
- Do not text long paragraphs to an angry person. They read them as an essay against them.

## Examples

**Partner, text, mid-fight:**
> I don't want to keep going like this over text. I'm sorry I snapped. Can we talk tonight when we're both home? I love you and I want to actually hear you.

**Friend, they feel let down:**
> I think I got defensive and that's not fair to you. You're right that I dropped the ball. Can I call you tomorrow and we sort it out properly?

**In person, script:**
> Okay. I'm getting heated and I don't want to say something I'll regret. I'm not walking away from this, I just need twenty minutes. I'll come find you.

**When they're angry and you don't yet understand why:**
> I can see you're really upset and I want to understand it. I'm not going to argue. Can you tell me what the worst part was?

**When you were actually wrong:**
> You're right. I was wrong about that and I'm sorry. Let me fix it.

## Avoid
- "You're overreacting."
- "I'm sorry you feel that way."
- "Fine." / "Whatever."
- "We've been over this."
- Bringing up a previous fight.
- Ultimatums in the heat of it.
- Long texts. Three sentences maximum.

## After the pause
Come back on time. Open with: "Okay, I've been thinking. Here's what I heard you say, tell me if I've got it wrong." Then listen before you respond. See `set-boundary` if the same fight keeps recurring.
