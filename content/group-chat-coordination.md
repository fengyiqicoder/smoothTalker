# Group chats: organising and deciding

## Why groups stall
Nobody replies because replying feels like committing, and everyone assumes someone else will. Fix: make the reply tiny, give a deadline, and name what happens if nobody answers.

## Principles
- **Propose, don't ask.** "Saturday 7pm at Luca's, who's in?" beats "what does everyone want to do?"
- **Two options, max.** A poll with two choices gets answered; one with six gets ignored.
- **Deadline with a default.** "I'll book for 6 on Thursday unless I hear otherwise."
- **Ask for a specific reaction.** "Thumbs up if in" makes it a one-tap reply.
- **Name people when you need them.** "@Sam @Priya you two haven't said" is fine if warm.
- **Move logistics to a smaller thread** once the plan is set.

## Examples

**Kick off a plan:**
> Right, birthday dinner for Jo. Proposal: Saturday 14th, 7:30, Bar Italia. 👍 if you're in, 👎 if you can't, by Wednesday please so I can book. Silence means I assume yes and you're paying for a chair.

**Chase a stalled decision:**
> Gentle nudge: still need to lock the date. Two options: the 14th or the 21st. Thumbs on the one you prefer. Deciding tomorrow night.

**Trip planning, first message:**
> Trip: I'm proposing Lisbon, 10 to 14 October, budget around $600 each all in. This is a real proposal, not a wish; if three people say yes by Friday I'll book the flat. Say yes, no, or "different dates".

**Someone keeps flaking, address in the group (light):**
> @Tom you're on your third "maybe next time", we're starting a tally. Genuinely, come Saturday, it's not the same without you.

**Someone keeps flaking, address privately (real):**
> Hey, not a big deal but you've bailed on the last few things and I'm starting to plan around you not coming. If things are hard right now, tell me and I'll stop pestering. If you just don't fancy the group stuff, that's fine too, I'd just rather know.

**Cutting through a long argument about where to eat:**
> Okay, executive decision: Thai place, 7:30. Anyone who objects can pick next time.

**Group chat that's become too noisy:**
> Suggestion: let's keep this one for plans and start a separate chat for the memes. I keep missing actual info.

**Leaving a plan you'd agreed to:**
> I've got to drop out of Saturday, sorry all. Don't change anything for me. Next one, I promise.

**Asking people to pay their share:**
> Booking's done, $85 each. My link is [link]; no rush but before the 10th please so I'm not out of pocket for weeks.

## Avoid
- "What does everyone think?"
- Open-ended questions with no deadline.
- Re-asking the same question when nobody replied; change the question.
- Passive-aggressive "guess nobody's interested then".
- Making the decision, then reopening it because one person complained.
- Chasing in the group when it's really about one person.
