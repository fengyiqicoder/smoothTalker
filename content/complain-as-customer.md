# Complaining as the customer

## Goal
The person reading it can solve it with the information in front of them, and wants to, because you were the reasonable one.

## Structure
1. **Order/reference number, date, what you bought.** First line.
2. **What went wrong, factually.** One or two sentences.
3. **What you've already tried** (if anything).
4. **What you want**, specifically: full refund, replacement, partial credit, an apology.
5. **A reasonable deadline.**
6. **Polite close, name, contact.**

## Principles
- Front-line agents can help you more if you are pleasant. Anger goes to the bottom of the pile or gets the script.
- Ask for one thing. "A refund or a replacement, your choice" is fine; a list of demands is not.
- Say what would make you happy. They often do not know.
- Keep the receipts: photos, screenshots, dates. Attach them.
- Escalate only if the first ask fails. Then: "I'd like this escalated to a manager" and restate.
- Mention your loyalty once if true ("I've ordered from you for years"). Not as a threat.

## Examples

**Wrong or damaged order, email or chat:**
> Hi,
> Order #58213, placed 4 June, arrived today with the blue jacket instead of the black one I ordered (photo attached). I'd like the black one sent out, and I'm happy to return the blue one with a prepaid label. Could you confirm by Friday? Thanks,
> Sam Lee, sam@email.com

**Faulty product, out of warranty edge:**
> The kettle I bought on 10 January (receipt attached) stopped working this week, so just under a year. I'd like a replacement or a refund. I've tried a different socket and a reset. Let me know what you need from me.

**Restaurant, after a bad experience (email to manager):**
> Hi, we ate at your Clapham branch on Saturday, table for four at 7:30. Two mains came out cold and one was wrong, and it took 25 minutes to get anyone's attention. Your staff were polite but clearly overstretched. I'm not looking for a fight; I'd appreciate a partial refund on the mains and I'd like to know it's been noted. Receipt attached.

**Airline / travel, cancellation:**
> Booking ref ABC123, flight LHR to JFK on 14 May, cancelled 3 hours before departure. I was rebooked to the next day and incurred a hotel night ($180, receipt attached). I'm requesting the compensation due under [regulation, if applicable] and reimbursement of the hotel. Please confirm within 14 days.

**Overcharged / double charged:**
> I was charged twice for order #58213 on 4 June ($89.00 each, statement attached). Please refund the duplicate charge. Let me know if you need anything else from me.

**Subscription you couldn't cancel:**
> I tried to cancel my subscription on the 2nd (screenshot attached) and was charged on the 5th anyway. Please cancel it, refund the $29 charge, and confirm no further charges will be taken.

**Escalation after a no:**
> Thanks for your reply. I don't think that resolves it, since [one reason]. I'd like this escalated to a manager. To restate: I'm asking for [X]. I'd rather settle this directly than through [card dispute / ombudsman / review], and I'm confident we can.

**In person / phone script:**
> Hi, I've got a problem with an order and I'm hoping you can help me sort it. [Facts]. What I'd like is [ask]. Is that something you can do?

## Avoid
- "This is the worst service I've ever had."
- Threatening a bad review or social media in the first message.
- ALL CAPS.
- A full timeline of your emotional journey.
- Asking for compensation wildly out of proportion.
- Not including the order number.
