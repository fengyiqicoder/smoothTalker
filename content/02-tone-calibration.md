# Tone calibration

Tone has three dials: **warmth**, **formality**, **length**. Set them from the relationship and the channel, then check against the other person's last message.

## By channel

| Channel | Length | Formality | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text / DM (iMessage, WhatsApp, Instagram) | 1 to 3 sentences | Low | Contractions, first names, one emoji at most if they use them. No "Dear". No sign-off. |
| Email | 3 to 6 sentences | Medium | Greeting, one-line context, the point, the ask, sign-off. Subject line does half the work. |
| Voice message or phone script | Conversational | Low to medium | Write it to be spoken. Short clauses. Pause markers. |
| Formal letter or platform review response | Structured | High | Full sentences, no slang, no exclamation marks. |

## Register markers by app

Each app has its own normal. Match it, then apply the mirror rule below.

| App | Length | Greeting and sign-off | Markers that read as normal | Markers that read as off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp | 1 to 3 lines; split long points into two messages | None, or their name | Voice notes, one emoji, "haha" | Blue-tick guilt ("I can see you read it"), paragraphs |
| iMessage / SMS | 1 to 2 lines | None | Lowercase, no full stop at the end, reactions instead of "ok" | A full stop after a one-word reply ("Fine.") reads cold |
| Slack / Teams | One message, not five in a row | "Hey [name]," or none | Threads for follow-ups, the ask in the first line, emoji reactions as acknowledgement | "Hi" then waiting for a reply before the question; @here for non-urgent things |
| LinkedIn DM | 3 to 5 sentences | "Hi [name]," and first name at the end | Why you, why now, one specific ask | A pitch in the first message, "I hope this finds you well" |
| Email | 3 to 6 sentences, one ask | "Hi [name]," / "Thanks," or "Best," | A subject line that says the point ("Invoice 1042, due Friday") | "Dear Sir/Madam" to a named person; five paragraphs for one question |
| Instagram / TikTok DM | 1 to 3 lines | None, or their handle's first name | Warm and quick, one emoji, a question to keep it going | Copy-pasted business email, long price lists in the first reply |
| WeChat (微信) | 1 to 3 lines; voice for close contacts only | 称呼 by role or name (王总, 李老师, 亲) | "好的", "收到" as acknowledgement, a polite emoji or 表情 with elders and clients | A 60-second voice message to a client or senior; a bare "嗯" or "哦" to someone senior |
| Xiaohongshu (小红书) DM or comment | 1 to 3 lines | "亲" or none | Friendly, emoji-rich, a question back | Hard selling or external links in the first reply |

## By relationship

| Relationship | Warmth | Formality | Default opener |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close friend or family | High | Low | Their name or nothing |
| Partner | High, careful | Low | Nothing; go straight in |
| Colleague, peer | Medium | Medium | "Hey [name]," |
| Boss or senior | Medium | Medium high | "Hi [name]," |
| Client or customer | Medium high | Medium | "Hi [name]," |
| Stranger, cold outreach | Medium | Medium | "Hi [name]," with immediate context |
| Someone who is angry at you | High, calm | Slightly higher than usual | Their name, then acknowledgement |

## The mirror rule

Read their last message. Copy their length within about 50 percent, copy their formality, copy their punctuation habits (do they use periods at the end of texts? exclamation marks? emoji?). Then move one notch warmer or clearer. Never jump two notches. A "Hey!! 😊" reply to a one-line period-ended text reads as fake.

## The same message at three settings

Situation: telling someone you can't make a meeting they proposed.

**Close friend, text:**
> Ah I can't do Thursday, I've got a thing till 7. Friday or the weekend?

**Colleague, chat:**
> Thursday doesn't work on my end, I'm double booked. Could we do Friday morning instead?

**Client, email:**
> Hi Dana,
> Thanks for suggesting Thursday. I have a conflict at that time, but Friday morning or any time Monday is open on my side. Would either of those work for you?
> Best,
> Sam

Notice: the reason gets shorter as the relationship gets closer, the alternative gets more specific as the stakes rise, and none of the three apologise.

## Common miscalibrations

- **Over-formal with peers**: "Per our discussion" to a teammate. Sounds like a threat.
- **Over-casual with a client after a mistake**: "oops my bad!" Sounds like you do not care.
- **Emoji to someone who never uses them**: reads as trying too hard.
- **Long message to a short question**: reads as defensive.
- **Warm sign-off after a firm boundary**: "Hope you understand!! xx" undercuts the boundary. Use a neutral close.
