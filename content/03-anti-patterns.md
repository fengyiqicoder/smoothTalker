# Anti-patterns

Smoothness fails in three directions: too slippery, too weak, too sharp. Each has tell-tale phrases. Remove them and replace as shown.

## Too slippery (reads as fake or salesy)

| Remove | Why it fails | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| "I hope this email finds you well" | Everyone knows it is filler | Delete, or one specific line ("Hope the launch went smoothly") |
| "I'd love to..." when you would not | False enthusiasm is detectable | "I can..." or "Happy to..." |
| "Amazing! So excited!!" | Inflated affect | One honest positive word |
| "Quick question" before a long one | Minimising the ask | State the ask plainly |
| "No pressure at all!" repeated | Over-signalling creates pressure | Say it once, or give a concrete out |
| "Let's circle back / touch base / synergy" | Corporate noise | Plain verbs: "let's talk Friday" |
| Compliment then immediate ask | Transparent setup | Ask first, or make the compliment specific and separate |

## Too weak (reads as anxious or low status)

| Remove | Why it fails | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| "Sorry to bother you" | Pre-apologising for existing | "Hi [name]," then the point |
| "I was just wondering if maybe" | Triple hedge | "Could you..." |
| "If that's okay?" after every sentence | Asking permission to speak | Once at the end, or not at all |
| "No worries if not!!" | Undercuts your own request | "If this week is full, next week works" |
| "I know you're busy but" | Tells them they should say no | Delete |
| Three exclamation marks | Nervous energy | Zero or one |
| "Sorry" more than once per message | Dilutes the real apology | Apologise once, then act |
| "Does that make sense?" | Invites them to say no | "Let me know if you want more detail" |

## Too sharp (reads as passive-aggressive or hostile)

| Remove | Why it fails | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| "Per my last email" | Everyone reads this as "you idiot" | "Resending the details below" |
| "As I mentioned" / "As you know" | Implies they forgot or are stupid | Just restate it |
| "Friendly reminder" | Never friendly | "Checking in on..." |
| "I'll wait" / "Thanks in advance" | Presumes compliance | "Would you be able to... by Friday?" |
| "With all due respect" | Signals disrespect | Delete; say the disagreement plainly |
| "Just to clarify" when correcting them | Condescending | "I think we may be reading this differently" |
| "Fine." / "Okay." / "K" | Cold shutdown | Either say what is wrong or a full sentence |
| "It's not my job to" | True but weaponised | "That sits with [team]; I can point you to them" |
| Ending a text with a period when they never do | Reads as anger | Match their punctuation |
| "?" alone or "??" | Impatience | "Did you get a chance to see this?" |

## Structural anti-patterns

- **Burying the ask** in paragraph four. Put it in the first two lines.
- **The sandwich that is all bread**: so much cushioning that the point is lost. One sentence of cushion is enough.
- **Explaining the whole history** before making the point. They were there.
- **Ending on a question you already know the answer to.** "Is that okay?" when it has to be okay.
- **Sending when angry.** If the user is heated, offer a version that waits, or a version that only says "I want to think about this and reply properly tomorrow."
