# Customer onboarding and welcome messages

## Goal
The customer feels sure they made a good choice, knows exactly what happens next, and sends you what you need without being chased. Most refund requests and angry emails start in the gap between paying and hearing anything useful.

Before they buy: `reply-to-customer-inquiry-dm`. When something goes wrong with the order: `shipping-delay-or-out-of-stock`.

## Structure
1. **Thank them by name**, for the specific thing they bought or booked.
2. **What happens next, with dates.** The next two or three steps, not the whole process.
3. **What you need from them**, as a short list with a deadline, and why.
4. **How to reach a person**, and your typical reply time.
5. **One small welcome touch** (a tip, a resource, what past clients found useful). Optional.

## Principles
- Send it fast. Within an hour of purchase for products, within a day for services. Silence after payment is the moment doubt starts.
- Confirm the concrete details: date, time, address, what is included. A confirmation that makes them re-check is a failed confirmation.
- Ask for everything you need once, in one list. Five separate emails asking for one thing each feels disorganised.
- Set expectations honestly, especially on timelines and response hours. Under-promise slightly, then deliver.
- Write as a person, not a system. Sign with a name.
- Keep the first message short. A long welcome pack can be a link or an attachment; the message itself says what they must do now.
- No upsell in the first message. Let them experience what they bought first.

## Examples

**Service business, after booking (email):**
> Hi Hannah, thanks for booking your kitchen consultation. I'm looking forward to it. Here's what happens next: I'll visit on Tuesday 14 May at 10am to measure, and you'll have three layout options by 24 May. Before the visit, could you send me photos of the current kitchen and any inspiration images by Friday? Any questions, reply here or text me on 07700 900123. I usually reply within a few hours on weekdays. Maria

**Freelancer, new client kickoff:**
> Welcome aboard, and thanks for choosing us for the rebrand. To start on Monday I need three things by Thursday: your current logo files, access to the brand folder, and the names of the two people who'll approve designs. First concepts land on 3 June. I'll send a short update every Friday so you always know where things are.

**Online shop, order confirmation:**
> Thanks for your order, Sam! Your candles are being poured this week and will ship by Thursday; you'll get a tracking link the moment they leave. Any problem, reply to this email and you'll get me, not a bot. Jade, Wick & Oak

**Membership or class, first message:**
> Welcome to the studio, Aisha! Your first class is Wednesday at 6:30pm. Come 10 minutes early so we can show you around, and bring water and a towel (mats are here). If you need to change the booking, you can do it in the app up to 12 hours before.

**Asking for missing information, gently:**
> Hi Dev, quick nudge on the onboarding form. I need it by Wednesday to keep your launch on 12 June. It takes about 10 minutes, link here. If anything on it is unclear, tell me and I'll fill in what I can.

**Setting availability and response-time expectations:**
> One practical note: I work Monday to Thursday and reply to email within one working day. For anything urgent on launch week, text me and I'll pick it up the same day.

## Avoid
- "Your order has been received" and nothing else.
- Asking for information piecemeal over several days.
- Promising timelines you cannot keep, to sound impressive.
- A no-reply address for a service where people will have questions.
- A welcome message that opens with terms and conditions or penalties.
- Upselling before they have received anything.

## If it goes badly
If they miss the deadline for what you need, send one friendly reminder with the concrete consequence ("otherwise launch moves to the 19th"), not a warning. If they come back anxious before you have started, answer quickly and restate the next step and date; reassurance is usually about uncertainty, not about you. If you realise you set an expectation you cannot meet, tell them as early as possible with a new date, before they have to ask.
> No problem at all. Just so you know, if I get the files by Monday we're still on track for the 12th; after that it moves to the 19th.
> Quick heads-up: the first concepts will be with you on 5 June rather than the 3rd. I'd rather tell you now than let it slip quietly.
