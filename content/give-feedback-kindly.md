# Giving honest feedback kindly

## Goal
They come away with something they can act on and they still trust you. Kindness without honesty is useless; honesty without kindness is not heard.

## Structure
1. **Ask what kind of feedback they want**, if unclear: "Do you want a cheerleader or an editor right now?"
2. **Start with what's genuinely working, specifically.** One or two things, real ones. This is calibration, not cushioning.
3. **The main issue, one thing, framed as a question or observation.** "The middle section lost me; I wasn't sure whose story it was."
4. **A concrete suggestion if you have one.** Otherwise, just the observation.
5. **Re-anchor on the goal**: "I'm saying this because I think this could be really good."

## Principles
- Most people can absorb one significant critique per sitting. Pick the most important.
- Describe your experience ("I got confused here") rather than pronounce a verdict ("this is confusing"). Experience cannot be argued with.
- Specific praise makes specific criticism credible. Generic praise ("it's great!") makes everything else suspect.
- If they did not ask, ask permission first. "Want a couple of thoughts, or are you happy with it?"
- Do not feedback the thing they cannot change (their voice, the deadline that passed).

## Examples

**Friend's writing:**
> I read it twice. The opening scene is properly good, I could see the kitchen. Where I got lost was the middle: three new characters arrive fast and I couldn't tell who to care about. If you cut one and gave the other two a line each that shows what they want, I think it'd snap into place. This is worth finishing.

**Friend's business idea:**
> I like the problem you're going after, it's real and I've felt it. My honest hesitation is the pricing: I'm not sure I'd pay monthly for it, though I'd pay once. Have you tested that with anyone yet? If it turns out I'm wrong, ignore me.

**Someone's presentation, before they give it:**
> The story in the middle about the customer is the strongest bit; I'd open with it. The first five slides are setup people already know. Also you say "basically" a lot when you're nervous; no one else will notice but you might.

**When it's genuinely not good and they asked for honesty:**
> You asked me to be straight so I will be. I don't think this version is there yet. The idea's strong; the execution feels rushed, mainly [one thing]. I'd rather tell you now than have you find out from someone who doesn't care about you.

**When they didn't ask:**
> Do you want thoughts, or are you done with it? Happy either way.

## Avoid
- The compliment sandwich with a fake bottom slice ("but overall great!!"). They only hear the middle and distrust the bread.
- "It's fine."
- Listing every small thing.
- Feedback on the person ("you're not a natural writer").
- "I would have done it differently."
- Delivering big feedback over text if they are anxious about it. Call.
