# Responding to a recruiter

## Goal
Every recruiter who contacts you leaves with a clear answer and a slightly better opinion of you. The good ones come back with better roles. The ones you decline stop chasing.

## Structure
1. **Thank them in five words or fewer.** "Thanks for reaching out."
2. **Your answer, in the first two lines.** Interested, not now, or no. Do not bury it.
3. **The one thing you need to move forward, if interested.** Usually the comp range, sometimes location or the team. Ask before booking a call.
4. **A concrete next step.** A time window, a request to stay in touch, or a clean close.

## Principles
- Reply to everyone you might want to hear from again. A two-line no costs nothing and keeps the channel warm.
- Ask for the range before the call. It is a normal question, and a recruiter who will not answer it is telling you something about the role.
- "Not now" needs a reason with a date attached, otherwise it reads as a soft no and they stop writing.
- You do not owe a reason for a no. "Not a fit for me" is a full sentence.
- LinkedIn replies are three sentences. Email replies are five. Save the paragraph for the call.
- Never bluff about competing offers or current comp. Recruiters compare notes.

## Examples

**Interested, asking for the range first, LinkedIn:**
> Thanks for reaching out, this sounds interesting. Before we book a call, could you share the base range and whether the role is remote? If those line up I'm free Thursday or Friday afternoon.

**Interested, email version:**
> Thanks for the note. I'm open to a conversation. Two questions before we set a time: what's the compensation band for the role, and is it fully remote or hybrid out of the Austin office? If those work for me, I can do a 30 minute call Thursday between 2 and 5 or Friday morning.

**Not now, keep in touch:**
> Thanks for thinking of me. I'm six months into a new role and not looking, but I'd like to hear from you again around March next year. Happy to connect here in the meantime.

**Not interested, LinkedIn length:**
> Thanks for reaching out. This one isn't a fit for me, but feel free to keep me in mind for senior platform roles in the future.

**Not interested, and you want them to stop:**
> Thanks, but I'm not looking and I'd rather not be contacted about roles. All the best with the search.

**Declining after learning about the role:**
> Thanks for walking me through it yesterday. Having thought it over, I'm going to pass. The role is more people management than I want right now. I appreciated your time, and if something more hands-on comes up I'd be glad to hear about it.

**Comp range is too low:**
> Thanks for sharing the band. It's below what I'd need to move, my floor is [$165k base]. If there's flexibility on that I'm happy to talk, otherwise no hard feelings and please keep me in mind for roles at that level.

**Following up when the recruiter goes quiet:**
> Hi [Name], checking in on the [Company] role. We spoke on the 3rd and you mentioned an update by the end of that week. Is the position still open? If it's moved on, no problem, just let me know so I can plan accordingly.

## Avoid
- Ignoring the message. Silence is a no with none of the upside.
- Sharing your current salary. Share your target, or ask for their range.
- Booking the call before you know the range, the location and the level.
- "I'm not really sure, maybe, let me think about it" with no date attached.
- Long explanations of why you are happy where you are. One line.
- Telling a recruiter you are "open to anything". You will get everything.

## If they push back

**They refuse to share the range before a call:**
> I understand. To save us both time, my floor for a move is [$165k base]. If the role is in that range, let's book the call. If not, I'll pass for now.

**They keep messaging after a no:**
> As I mentioned, I'm not looking. Please remove me from your outreach list. Thanks.
Then stop replying. Two clear messages is enough.

**They go quiet after your follow-up:**
Send one more, a week later, then assume the role is gone and move on.
> Last check on the [Company] role. If it's closed out, no reply needed. Thanks for the conversation either way.
