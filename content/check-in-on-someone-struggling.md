# Checking in on someone who is struggling

## Goal
They feel seen without feeling like a project. The message asks nothing of them, gives them an easy way to respond or not, and leaves a door open they can walk through later.

## First check-in
1. **Say why you're writing, in one line.** "I heard about the redundancy" or "You've been quiet and I've been thinking about you."
2. **No questions that need a report.** "How are you?" asks them to summarise a bad month. Prefer "no need to reply" or a question with a small answer.
3. **One concrete offer** (see below). Not a menu.
4. **Release them.** "No pressure to answer this."

## When they don't reply
1. Wait at least a few days. Silence is not rejection, it is often the only energy they have.
2. Send something that needs zero response: a photo, a memory, a "still here."
3. Never mention the unanswered message. "Did you get my text?" turns your care into a debt.
4. Keep the rhythm gentle: once a week or so, not daily, unless you are genuinely worried about their safety.

## When they say "I'm fine"
1. Take it at face value the first time. Pushing makes the next "fine" more defended.
2. Leave the door propped: "Okay. If fine stops being true, you don't need a reason to text me."
3. If the behaviour and the word clearly disagree, name it once, gently, and stop.

## Offering concrete help
"Let me know if you need anything" hands them a task most people in a hole will not do. Offer one specific thing with a time attached, that they only have to accept or decline: food, a lift, a walk, a school pickup.

## Principles
- Their pace, not yours. You are a lamp in the hallway, not a search party.
- Presence beats advice. Do not fix, reframe, or find the silver lining.
- Shorter is kinder. A long message asks for a long reply.
- Be consistent more than intense. Three small texts across a month do more than one essay.
- Professional help gets one warm line, offered as an option, never as a verdict on them. You do not name conditions and you do not diagnose.

## Examples

**Friend went quiet:**
> Hey, you've gone a bit quiet and I've been thinking about you. No need to reply to this. I'm around all weekend if you want company, or silence in the same room, either is fine.

**Job loss:**
> Heard about Tuesday. That's rubbish and it's not a verdict on you. I'm making a big pot of curry Thursday, can I drop some at yours around 7?

**Breakup:**
> Sorry about you and [name]. I'm not going to ask how you're doing because I think the answer is obvious. Do you want to walk the dog with me Saturday morning, 10ish? Zero talking required.

**Burnout:**
> You've been running on empty for months and I can see it. I'm not going to tell you to rest, you've heard it. Can I take [the kids / the dog / the airport run] off your hands on Sunday so you get an actual day?

**Depression, they've been withdrawn:**
> Still here. No pressure, no questions. Sending a picture of the dog being an idiot. Text me one letter if you want me to come over.

**Illness, longer haul:**
> Thinking of you before the scan on Monday. I'll text at 3 just to say hi, you don't have to answer. And I've got Wednesday evening free if you want me to bring dinner and a bad film.

**Pointing to professional help, one line:**
> I'm always going to be here, and I also think you deserve more support than a mate with a curry. If you ever want a hand finding someone to talk to, I'll help you look, no judgment, just an offer.

**After "I'm fine":**
> Okay, I believe you. And if fine stops being true at 2am, you can text me without a reason. Same offer stands for the walk on Saturday.

## Avoid
- "Everything happens for a reason", "at least", "you're so strong", "have you tried".
- Comparing to your own hard time unless they ask.
- Asking for updates. They are not obliged to report.
- Sending a check-in and then going silent when they finally open up.
- Naming a condition. "You sound depressed" is a diagnosis, and it is not yours to make.

## If it goes badly
If they snap at you or push you away:
> That's fair, I'll back off. The offer doesn't expire. I'm not going anywhere.
Then do exactly that, without defending your intent. Reappear in a couple of weeks with something small.

If you are genuinely worried about their safety, say it plainly and stay on the line:
> I'm worried about you and I'd rather ask than wonder. Are you safe right now? I can come over, or sit on the phone with you while you call [crisis line for their country].
Asking directly does not put the idea in their head, and it tells them they can talk about it. If they say they might act on it, or you cannot reach them and fear for their life, call your local emergency number. That one is not yours to carry alone.
