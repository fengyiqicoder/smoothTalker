# Team announcements as a manager

## Goal
Everyone on the team knows what changed, what it means for their own work, and where to take questions, before the rumours fill the gap. People forgive bad news far more readily than they forgive finding out sideways or feeling spun.

Telling one person bad news: `deliver-bad-news`. Telling customers: `price-increase-and-customer-announcements`.

## Structure
1. **Lead with the news** in the first sentence. No "exciting updates" preamble for something that is not exciting.
2. **The reason, briefly and truthfully.** One or two sentences.
3. **What it means for them.** Their work, their manager, their pay or their hours, whatever they will actually worry about.
4. **What happens next**, with dates.
5. **Where to take questions**, and a real offer of a 1:1.

## Principles
- Tell the affected people first, individually, then the team. Nobody should learn about their own job change from a group post.
- Tell the team before the wider company when you can, and at roughly the same moment for everyone, so no one hears it from a colleague.
- Match the channel to the weight. Hard news goes in a live meeting or call, followed by a written summary. Routine news can be a message.
- Say what you do not know yet, and when you will. "I don't know yet, I'll update you by Friday" builds more trust than a vague reassurance.
- Do not dress up bad news. "Streamlining", "right-sizing" and "exciting journey" land as insults on a hard day.
- Respect the person leaving. Share only what they agreed you can share, and never hint at why.
- Own the decision you are part of. "Leadership decided" distances you; if you agreed with it, say so.

## Examples

**Someone leaving (with their agreement on wording):**
> Some news: Ana's last day will be 28 March. She's moving to a product role at a climate startup, and we're all a bit jealous. Ana, thank you for three years of making our releases calmer. Leo will pick up the billing integrations; I'll share a handover plan by Friday. Let's give her a proper send-off next Thursday.

**New hire joining:**
> Please welcome Tomás, who joins as a senior designer on Monday. He's coming from eight years in fintech and will own the onboarding flow. Say hi, and grab 15 minutes with him in his first week if you can.

**Reorg, written follow-up after the meeting:**
> As we discussed this morning: from 1 May, the support and success teams become one team under Priya. Your role, pay and day-to-day work stay the same for now. What changes is who you report to and our weekly rhythm. Priya and I will each hold 1:1s with everyone in the next two weeks. Questions to me or Priya anytime, including the awkward ones.

**Layoffs, to the remaining team:**
> This morning we let go of six colleagues across sales and ops, including Mark and Dee from our team. It's a hard day, and I want you to hear it from me directly. The reason is that revenue came in well below plan and we had to cut costs. No further cuts are planned for our team. I know that's hard to hear after today, so I'll be straight with you if anything changes. My door is open all day.

**Missed target:**
> We finished Q2 at 78% of our target. That's a miss, and I own my part in how we planned it. Two things drove it: the delayed launch and a slower renewal cycle. Here's what changes for Q3: fewer priorities, a mid-quarter check-in, and I'll share the revised plan on Monday. Good work doesn't disappear because a number was missed.

**New policy people will not love:**
> From 1 June, the company is moving to three office days a week, Tuesday to Thursday. I know this is a big change for some of you, especially anyone with a long commute or caring duties. If it creates a real problem for you, come and talk to me this week and we'll look at options together.

## Avoid
- Opening with "exciting news" when it is not.
- Announcing a departure or reorg before telling the people it affects most.
- "Nothing is changing" when something is.
- Long paragraphs of context before the actual news.
- Taking questions only in public. Some people will only ask privately.
- Speculating about the person who left, or letting others speculate in the thread.

## If it goes badly
If the thread turns angry or anxious, do not argue in public. Acknowledge the feeling in one line, answer the factual questions you can, and move the rest to 1:1s. If you got something wrong in the announcement, correct it quickly and plainly in the same channel. If you were asked to share something you believe is misleading, push back before the announcement, not after (see `push-back-on-boss`).
> I hear you, and it's fair to be frustrated. I'll answer what I can here and I'm booking 1:1s with everyone this week for the rest.
> Correction to my earlier message: the change starts on 1 June, not 1 May. Sorry for the confusion.
