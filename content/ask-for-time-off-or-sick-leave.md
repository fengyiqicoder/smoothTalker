# Asking for time off or sick leave

## Goal
The manager knows the dates, knows the work is covered, and can say yes in one word. You give enough information to plan around and nothing they have to hold in confidence.

## Structure
1. **The dates, first line.** "I'd like to take 14 to 18 October off." Not "I was wondering if it might be possible".
2. **One sentence on coverage.** Who has what, or what you will finish before you go.
3. **The category, if it helps them.** Vacation, sick, bereavement, parental. Not the diagnosis, not the family story.
4. **The ask, closed.** "Can you approve?" or "I'll put it in the system today."
5. **For same-day absence, skip steps 1 to 4 and send three lines:** not coming in, what is urgent today and who has it, when you will next check in.

## Principles
- Vacation is a notification with a courtesy question attached. You are entitled to it. Sick leave is a notification, full stop.
- Detail is not a favour to your manager. "I'm unwell" is complete. "Stomach thing, been up all night" makes both of you uncomfortable and adds nothing.
- Mental-health days are sick days. You do not need to label them differently unless your company culture rewards it.
- Ask early for planned leave, ideally before anyone else books the same week. Ask the same morning for illness, before your start time if you can.
- The handover line is what makes the yes easy. Write it before you write the request.
- For parental leave, give the news and the rough timeline in the same message so your manager can start planning instead of guessing.

## Examples

**Vacation request, email or Slack:**
> I'd like to take 14 to 18 October off, back on the 21st. The Hendricks deliverable is due the 11th so it'll be done before I go, and Priya has agreed to cover the Tuesday client call. Can you approve?

**Same-day sick text to manager:**
> I'm unwell and won't be in today. The only urgent thing is the 2pm review, I've asked Dan to run it and sent him the notes. I'll check messages once mid-afternoon and update you tomorrow morning.

**Mental-health day, when you want to say so:**
> I'm taking a sick day tomorrow, I need a day to reset. Nothing on my plate is blocking anyone before Thursday. Back Wednesday.

**Bereavement leave:**
> My grandfather died last night. I'll need to be out this week, likely through Friday, for the funeral and family. Ana has the Q4 numbers and knows where everything is. I'll confirm my return date by Wednesday.

**Parental leave announcement:**
> Some news: we're expecting, due mid-March. I'm planning to take [12] weeks from around the due date. I know that's a while away, so I'd like to start a handover plan in January. Can we book 30 minutes to talk it through next week?

**Leaving early today:**
> I need to leave at 3 today for a [school pickup / appointment]. I'll finish the draft before I go and I'm reachable on my phone if anything comes up.

**Longer trip, asking well ahead:**
> Heads up for planning: I'd like three weeks off, 2 to 20 June. That's before the summer release cycle, and I'd handle the Kestrel migration before I leave. Are those dates workable from your side?

## Avoid
- "I was wondering if it would be at all possible, if it's not too inconvenient..."
- Medical detail. Symptoms, appointments, test names. None of it.
- Asking for sick leave as a question. "Would it be okay if I stayed home?" invites a no to something that is not theirs to refuse.
- Promising to work while sick. "I'll be online all day" turns sick leave into remote work with a temperature.
- Apologising for being ill or for taking holiday you have earned.
- Booking the flights before the dates are approved, then presenting them as fixed.

## If they push back

**Vacation dates are inconvenient:**
> Understood. Which week nearby works better for the team? I can move it to the 21st if the launch is the concern, but I'd like to lock something in this week so I can book.

**They ask for a doctor's note:**
> Sure. Policy says a note is needed after [three] days, so I'll get one if I'm out past Wednesday. If you need one for a shorter absence, let me know and I'll ask the GP for a letter.
If the request is out of step with the written policy, ask them to point you to the policy rather than arguing. Check with HR if you cannot find it.

**They ask what is wrong:**
> Nothing I need help with, just unwell. I'll be back as soon as I'm fit to work.

**Silence on a leave request:**
> Following up on my leave for 14 to 18 October. If I don't hear otherwise by Friday I'll assume it's fine and put it in the system.
