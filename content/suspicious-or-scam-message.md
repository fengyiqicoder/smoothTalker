# Replying to a suspicious or scam message

## Goal
You lose nothing, the real person behind a hacked account gets warned, and any relative caught in a scam stays close enough to you to accept help. The scammer gets nothing, including your attention.

## Core rule
**Verify through a channel you already had, never through the one the message gives you.** Call the number saved in your phone, open the bank's app yourself, log in to the courier's site by typing the address. A scammer controls the message; they do not control your existing contacts.

## Structure
1. **Do not act inside the message.** No links, no codes, no "confirm your details", no replying to unknown numbers.
2. **Check the channel.** Does the ask involve money, a code, urgency, secrecy or a link? Two of those is enough to treat it as a scam.
3. **Verify outside it.** Phone call to a saved number, a question only the real person could answer, or the official app.
4. **If real, help. If not, block, report, and warn the real person** through the verified channel.
5. **Delete it.** Do not keep a thread open "in case".

## Principles
- Not replying is a complete response. Scammers work lists; a reply, even "wrong number", marks your number as live.
- Never send a code. A real friend does not need your verification code for anything. This one rule stops most account takeovers.
- Urgency is the tell. Anyone genuine can wait ten minutes for you to call them back.
- Relatives who are being scammed are already ashamed. Shame makes them hide it. Your job is to be the person they can tell.
- Your bank will never ask you to move money to a "safe account", and no courier withholds a parcel for a small fee by text.

## Examples

**"Friend" on WhatsApp asks for money or a code (do not reply there; call the real number):**
> Hey, just got a message from your WhatsApp asking me for a code. I'm guessing that wasn't you? Ring me when you can.

**Warning the real friend when you confirm the account is hacked (by phone, SMS or another app):**
> Your Instagram is sending "I need a favour" messages to people. It's not you, right? Change the password and turn on two-step, and tell your followers from another account so nobody sends money.

**"Bank" or "delivery" text with a link:**
Send nothing. Open the bank app or type the courier site yourself. To report it, forward the text to your carrier's spam number or your bank's fraud line, then delete it.

**Wrong-number opener from a stranger ("Hi, is this David from the gym?"):**
Send nothing. If it is a real wrong number, they will find the right one. If you must, one line and then block:
> Wrong number.

**Too-good job offer by DM ("$4,000 a week, no experience, reply YES"):**
Send nothing. Look up the company yourself and message its official careers address if curious:
> Hi, I received a message claiming to be from your recruiting team about a remote position. Can you confirm whether it's genuine?

**A relative's account seems hacked (message them on another channel you already had):**
> Auntie, your Facebook is sending odd messages about a grant. I don't think it's you, so I've not replied. Can I call you tonight and we'll change the password together?

**Telling a relative you think they are being scammed, without shaming them:**
> Mum, I've been thinking about that man from the trading group you mentioned. Can I ask something, and I promise I'm not going to lecture: has he asked you to send money or keep it quiet? These people are professionals and they fool clever people every day. Whatever the answer is, we'll sort it out together.

**Acquaintance says they sent money before realising:**
> Don't beat yourself up, these are designed to work. Call your bank's fraud line right now and tell them exactly what happened; the sooner you call, the better the chance of a recall. Then report it to [national fraud service]. I'm around if you want company on the call.

## Avoid
- Clicking to "just see" what the link is.
- Replying "STOP" to unknown numbers, which confirms yours is live.
- Sending any code, even "just this once".
- Arguing with, mocking or baiting the scammer.
- "How could you fall for that?" to a relative. It ends the conversation and the next scam goes unreported.

## If it goes badly
If the relative gets defensive:
> You're right, it's your money and your decision. I'm not going to nag. I just want you to know that if anything about it starts to feel off, you can tell me and I won't say I told you so.
Then check in again in a week. If they have already sent money, help them call the bank first and talk about it later. If a stranger keeps messaging after you ignore them, block and report; do not send a final reply.
