# Asking someone to change a behaviour

## Goal
They change the specific thing, and the relationship is not damaged. Being right is not the goal.

## Structure
1. **Low-key opener** that signals this is small and friendly.
2. **The specific behaviour**, observable, no character judgement. "The dishes have been sitting two or three days" not "you're messy."
3. **The effect on you**, brief, first person.
4. **The concrete ask**: what you want instead, specifically.
5. **Something that makes it easy to say yes** (flexibility, acknowledgement of their side).

## Principles
- One behaviour per conversation. A list becomes an attack.
- Raise it early, when it is a small annoyance. Waiting until you're furious guarantees a bad conversation.
- Frame the ask as a shared system, not a correction. "Can we do X?" beats "Can you stop Y?"
- Do it in person or voice if you live together. Text for neighbours you barely know.
- Assume they do not know it bothers you. They usually do not.

## Examples

**Roommate, dishes, in person or text:**
> Hey, small thing: the dishes have been piling up for a few days at a time and it's stressing me out when I cook. Could we go with a "same day" rule? I'm happy to do the same and I'll hold myself to it.

**Roommate, noise at night:**
> Can I ask a favour? I've got early starts this month and the TV through the wall after 11 is keeping me up. Would headphones after 11 be doable? I'll do the same on weekends when you're sleeping in.

**Neighbour you don't know, note or message:**
> Hi, I'm in flat 4. Your music's been coming through pretty loud most nights after 10; I don't think you'd know from your side. Would you mind keeping it lower after 10 on weeknights? Thanks so much, and shout if anything from our side ever bothers you.

**Friend who's always late:**
> Can I be honest about something small? You're usually 20 or 30 minutes late and I end up sitting around. It's not a big deal, but could you text me when you're actually leaving? Then I'll time it right and neither of us is stuck waiting.

**Partner, a habit:**
> Can I bring up a tiny thing without it being a thing? When you leave your phone on loud at night it wakes me every time it buzzes. Could it go on silent after we're in bed?

**Repeated after you've asked once:**
> I mentioned the dishes a couple of weeks ago and it's slipped back. I'm not trying to nag; it genuinely affects me. Can we figure out something that'll actually stick? Maybe a rota, or I'll take dishes and you take bins?

## Avoid
- "You always" / "you never."
- Bringing it up in front of others.
- Saving up five things.
- Passive-aggressive notes or sighing.
- Apologising for having the request.
- Threats ("or I'm moving out") unless you mean it.
