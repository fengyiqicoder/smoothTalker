# Reconnecting after silence

## Goal
Make the gap a non-issue in one sentence, then give them something specific and easy to respond to.

## Structure
1. **Name the gap lightly**, once. Do not apologise for it at length; they went quiet too.
2. **A specific reason you thought of them** (a memory, something you saw, a real update).
3. **One easy question** about them, or one low-stakes invitation.
4. **No pressure to reply fast.**

## Principles
- Most people are relieved, not annoyed, when an old friend reaches out. The awkwardness is mostly yours.
- Do not open with a favour. If you need something, reconnect first, ask in a later message or say the ask plainly and separately.
- Do not try to catch up on everything in the first message.

## Examples

**Old friend, text:**
> It's been about two years, which is absurd. I walked past that ramen place we used to go to and thought of you. How are things? No rush, just wanted to say hi.

**Former colleague, LinkedIn or email:**
> Hi Ana, long time. I saw the news about your move to [company] and wanted to say congrats, it looks like a great fit. If you're ever up for a coffee, I'd love to hear how it's going.

**Someone you drifted from after a falling out (no big conversation, just warmth):**
> Hey. I've been thinking about you lately and hoping you're doing well. No agenda. If you ever want to grab a coffee, I'd like that.

**Reconnecting because you need something (be honest):**
> I'll be upfront that I'm reaching out because I could use your advice on [X], and I feel a bit sheepish since we haven't talked in ages. Either way, I've thought about you and hope you're well. If you have 20 minutes some time, I'd be grateful; if not, no hard feelings at all.

## Avoid
- "Sorry I've been such a bad friend" (makes them console you).
- A wall of updates about your life.
- "We should catch up sometime!" with nothing concrete.
- Reconnecting only when you need something, without acknowledging it.

## If they reply warmly
Suggest one specific thing within two messages: a call this week, a coffee on a named day. Momentum dies otherwise.
