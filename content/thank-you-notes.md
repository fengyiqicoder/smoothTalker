# Thank-you notes

## Goal
The person feels that you noticed exactly what they did and that it mattered. A good thank-you is short, specific and a little bit personal. The formula is easy; what makes it land is one concrete detail they could not have guessed you would mention.

For responding to praise, or quick appreciation at work, see `give-and-receive-compliments`.

## Structure
1. **Thank them for the specific thing**, by name. "The blue linen tablecloth," not "the lovely gift."
2. **Say what it did for you or how you will use it.** One real detail.
3. **Mention the person, not only the object or favour.** Their effort, their thought, the time they gave.
4. **A forward line.** When you will see them, how you will pass it on, or what happens next.
5. **Warm sign-off.**

## Principles
- Specific beats effusive. One real detail is worth three adjectives.
- Send it soon. Within a few days for texts and emails, two weeks for cards. Late is still far better than never.
- For money gifts, name what you will do with it rather than the amount.
- If you did not love the gift, thank them for the thought and the care, honestly. Never invent enthusiasm you will have to keep up.
- Handwritten for weddings, funerals, mentors, formal gifts and older relatives. A text is fine for friends, dinner and small favours.
- Match the length to what they did. A lift to the airport gets two lines; a year of mentoring earns a paragraph.

## Examples

**Gift, from a friend (text):**
> Thank you for the ceramic mug! It's already my morning coffee mug and I think of you every time I pick it up. Brunch soon? My shout.

**Host after a dinner or weekend (text):**
> Thanks so much for having us on Saturday. That lamb was ridiculous and the kids are still talking about the treehouse. Our turn next, how's the 20th?

**Money gift, from a relative (card):**
> Dear Aunt Mei, thank you so much for the birthday money. It's going towards the pottery course I've been wanting to take for ages, so you'll get a slightly wonky bowl out of it. Love, Lin

**Wedding gift (card):**
> Dear Sophie and Raj, thank you for the beautiful serving board. It had pride of place at our first dinner as a married couple. More than that, thank you for travelling so far to be with us; it meant a lot to see you there.

**Mentor or former manager (email):**
> I wanted to say thank you properly. The way you walked me through that first client pitch, and let me run the second one, is the reason I felt ready for this new role. I hope I get to do the same for someone one day. Coffee next time I'm in town?

**Someone who did a big favour:**
> Thank you for looking after Biscuit all week. Coming home to a happy dog and a note about his new favourite stick made my day. Please let me return the favour whenever you need it.

**Very late thanks:**
> This is embarrassingly late, but I didn't want it to go unsaid: thank you for the baby blanket. It's become Leo's favourite and it's in half our photos. We're so grateful.

**A gift you didn't love:**
> Thank you for thinking of me with the candle set, that was really kind, and it was lovely to see you at the party.

## Avoid
- "Thanks for the gift" with no detail. It reads as a template.
- Over-apologising for lateness. One clause, then the thanks.
- Mentioning the price or the amount of money.
- Sliding in an ask at the end ("also, could you...").
- Copying the same message to every guest. Change at least the specific line.
- Promising to use something you will not use.

## If it goes wrong
If you realise you have forgotten to thank someone for months, send it now with one line of ownership, not a paragraph; it will still be welcome. If you thanked the wrong person for a gift, correct it lightly and thank both. If you cannot remember who gave what, ask the organiser or check the cards before guessing, and send a general warm thanks to anyone you truly cannot place.
> I've just realised I never properly thanked you for the housewarming plant, which is shameful given it's thriving. Thank you!
> Small mix-up: I thanked Dan for your lovely book, when it was you. Thank you, I'm halfway through already.
