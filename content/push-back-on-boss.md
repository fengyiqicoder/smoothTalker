# Pushing back on your boss

## Goal
Your manager sees you as someone who raises problems early with options attached. Not someone who says no, and not someone who silently drowns.

## Structure
1. **Align with the goal.** "I want to get this done well."
2. **The constraint, factual.** "Right now I have A, B and C due this week."
3. **The trade-off, made visible.** "If I take this on, one of them slips."
4. **Options, two or three, with your recommendation.**
5. **Ask them to choose.** This is the key move: you are not refusing, you are asking them to prioritise.

## Principles
- Never a bare no. Always "here's what it would take" or "here's what has to give."
- Bring the trade-off to them; do not make it yourself and hope they do not notice.
- Raise it as early as possible. Pushback on day one is planning; pushback on the deadline is failure.
- Be specific about time. "This is a two-day job" beats "this is a lot."
- Written follow-up after a verbal conversation, so the decision is on record.

## Examples

**Unrealistic deadline, chat or email:**
> I can get this done, but not to the standard we'd want by Thursday. To do it properly is about three days. Options: I deliver a rough version Thursday and the polished one Monday; or I push the Marsh report to next week and give this my full time. My recommendation is the first. Which do you prefer?

**Too much on your plate:**
> Quick priority check. I've got the launch deck, the Q3 numbers and the onboarding doc all due this week and I can realistically do two well. Which one can move? My suggestion is the onboarding doc, since it's internal.

**Disagreeing with a decision:**
> Can I flag a concern before we commit? I think [decision] risks [specific problem] because [reason]. If we've already weighed that, I'll get on board. If not, [alternative] might get us the same result with less exposure.

**Being asked to do something outside your role, repeatedly:**
> I've been picking up the invoicing for a few months now and I'm happy to help in a pinch, but it's now about four hours a week that's coming out of [core work]. Can we either make it official and rebalance, or move it back to finance?

**Pushback on a weekend request:**
> I can't do this weekend, I've got commitments I can't move. I can start first thing Monday and have it to you by noon. If it truly can't wait, let's talk about what else comes off my plate.

**Written follow-up after agreeing:**
> Just confirming what we agreed: I'll deliver the rough version Thursday and the final on Monday, and the Marsh report moves to the 14th. Shout if I've got any of that wrong.

## Avoid
- "That's not my job."
- "I'm too busy."
- Saying yes and then missing.
- Complaining about workload without a proposed fix.
- Pushing back in front of their boss or the team.
- Making the decision for them silently.
