# Negotiating price, rate or salary

## Goal
You get closer to your number and the other side still wants to work with you. The tone is collaborative: "how do we make this work" not "give me more".

## Structure
1. **Enthusiasm first, genuine and short.** They need to know you want this.
2. **The gap, factually.** "The offer is at X; based on [market/scope/my last role], I was expecting closer to Y."
3. **Your number, specific.** Not a range (they will take the bottom). Slightly above what you would accept.
4. **One reason**, the strongest, framed as value to them.
5. **Collaborative ask.** "Is there flexibility there?" or "What would it take to get to Y?"
6. **Optional: name what else could move** (start date, equity, title, scope, payment terms) so a no on money is not a dead end.

## Principles
- Never apologise for negotiating. It is expected; not negotiating is the surprise.
- Silence after your number is fine. Do not fill it by lowering your number.
- Anchor with a number; "more" is not a negotiation.
- Reasons about your needs (rent, kids) are weak. Reasons about your value or the market are strong.
- Be the person they want to say yes to. Warm, calm, unhurried.
- Get it in writing before you decline anything else.

## Examples

**Job offer, email:**
> Hi Jordan,
> Thank you for the offer, I'm genuinely excited about the role and the team. I want to be straightforward about one thing: the base is at $95k, and based on the scope we discussed and what I'm seeing for comparable roles, I was expecting closer to $110k. Is there flexibility to get there? If base is fixed, I'd be open to talking about a signing bonus or an earlier review. Either way I'm keen to make this work.
> Best,
> Alex

**Freelance rate, client pushes back:**
> I understand the budget's tight. My rate is $120/hour and that's where I need to be for this kind of work. What I can do is scope it tighter: if we drop [X], I can bring the total under your number. Would that work?

**Client asks for a discount:**
> I don't discount the rate, but I'm happy to find a version that fits your budget. What if we phase it: [smaller scope] now at [price], and the rest when it makes sense?

**Buying something, private seller:**
> I'm interested and can collect this week. Would you take £350? I know it's under asking; that's what I can do in cash today.

**Raise conversation, script:**
> I'd like to talk about my compensation. Over the last year I've [two specific results]. I've looked at the market for this role and I'm below it. I'd like to move to [number]. What would it take to get there?

**They say no:**
> Understood. Can we agree a date to revisit, say six months, with clear goals attached? And in the meantime, is there movement on [other lever]?

## Avoid
- "I was hoping for a little more, if possible?"
- Giving a range.
- Justifying with personal expenses.
- Ultimatums you will not follow through on.
- Accepting on the call. "Let me review the full package and come back to you tomorrow" is always fine.
- Negotiating twice on the same point after they have moved once.
