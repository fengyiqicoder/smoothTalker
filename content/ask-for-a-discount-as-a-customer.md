# Asking a vendor for a discount, price match or fee waiver

## Goal
You get a lower price, a waived fee or a perk, and the seller feels they did a reasonable thing for a reasonable person. The ask is specific, based on something true, and easy to say yes to. If they say no, you have lost nothing and they still like you.

## Structure
1. **Signal you want to buy.** "I'm ready to book." Sellers discount buyers, not browsers.
2. **One anchoring fact.** A competitor's real quote, your volume, your history with them, paying upfront, flexible timing, an off-peak date, a fee you did not expect.
3. **The specific ask.** A number, a percentage, a named perk, or "match this quote". Not "can you do anything on price?"
4. **Make yes easy.** Say what happens the moment they agree: "I'll pay the deposit today."
5. **A face-saving out.** "If that's not possible, I understand."

## Principles
- Facts beat feelings. "Your competitor quoted $1,400" works. "Money's tight" makes them uncomfortable and rarely moves the number.
- Never invent a competitor quote. It is dishonest, and sellers know their market well enough to catch it. No quote? Get one, or use another lever.
- Ask for a specific number, slightly ambitious, so they can meet you partway and feel they won.
- If price is fixed, ask for perks: delivery, a longer warranty, an upgrade, an extra month, waived setup.
- Ask the person who can say yes: a manager, owner or retention team.
- Timing: end of month for dealers, off-season for wedding vendors, the renewal window for SaaS.
- Keep it brief and pleasant. It is a normal thing normal people say.
- When they say no, thank them and decide. Sulking costs the goodwill you need for the next ask.

## Examples

**Local shop, in person or DM:**
> I'd like to take the two chairs and the side table together. Is there any room on price if I take all three today? I was thinking $520 for the set.

**Competitor quote, email to a vendor:**
> Hi Rachel, I'd like to go with you for the kitchen fitting. One thing: [other firm] quoted $6,800 for the same scope, and yours is $7,400. If you can meet them at $6,800, or get close, I'll sign and pay the deposit this week. If not, tell me what's different about your quote and I'll take it into account.
> Ben

**Car dealer, email before visiting:**
> Hi Marcus, I'm buying a [model] this month and yours is one of two I'm considering. Your listed price is $24,900. I can pay in full on Friday if you can do $23,500 out the door, including fees. If that works, I'll come in Friday morning to finish the paperwork.

**Wedding vendor:**
> Hi Ivy, we love your work and want to book you for 14 February. Our photography budget is $2,800 and your package is $3,200. Two things that might help: it's a Thursday, and we only need six hours rather than eight. Would $2,800 for six hours work? If yes, I'll send the deposit today.

**SaaS renewal:**
> Hi, our renewal is coming up at $4,200 for 12 seats. We've been on the plan three years and we're staying. Is there a loyalty or annual-prepay rate? I'd be looking for around $3,600, or the same price with four extra seats. I can sign this week if so.

**Hotel, the day before arrival:**
> We're arriving tomorrow for four nights and it's our anniversary. If any upgraded rooms are free, we'd be grateful for one. No problem at all if not.

**Fee waiver, bank or utility:**
> Hi, I've been charged a $35 late fee. I've had this card six years and this is the first late payment; it was a bank holiday and it cleared the next morning. Could you waive it this once? I've set up autopay so it won't happen again.

**Landlord's agent, before renewing:**
> Hi Dana, I'd like to renew for another year. The proposed rent is $1,950. Comparable two-beds in the building are listed at $1,850 on [site] right now, and I've paid on time for three years with no repair callouts. Would you renew at $1,850? I can sign this week if so.

**Price match, retail:**
> I'd like to buy this from you rather than online. [Retailer] has it at $189 today, here's the link. Can you match that? If yes I'll take it now.

## Avoid
- Inventing a competitor quote or a budget you do not have.
- "Can you do anything on price?" with no number.
- Pleading or sad stories with no other lever.
- Threatening a bad review to get a discount.
- Asking twice after a clear no.
- Insulting the product to justify the ask.

## If they say no
Thank them and either buy at their price or walk, no drama. "Understood, thanks for checking. I'll go ahead at $7,400 then, could you send the deposit details?" If they say no to price, try one perk before you close: "If the price is firm, could you include delivery?" If the gap is too large, walk and leave the door open: "That's beyond me right now, but I appreciate your time. If anything changes on your side, I'd still like to work with you."
