# Landlords, tenants, contractors and service providers

## Goal
Get the outcome while keeping the relationship workable, because you will need this person again. Everything in writing, everything dated, everything calm.

## Principles
- Written, always. Text or email, never only a call. If you call, follow up with "confirming what we discussed".
- Facts, dates, amounts. Feelings weaken these messages.
- Ask for a specific thing by a specific date.
- Reference the agreement or the law once, neutrally, not as a threat. "Under the lease" is enough.
- Stay pleasant. The person who stays pleasant and precise wins most disputes.
- Escalate in steps: request → reminder with date → formal notice. Never jump to the last step first.

## Rent increase, pushing back

**Do not send a flat no.** A flat refusal to a rent increase usually ends in non-renewal. The move is a counter: a specific number, one or two strong reasons, and an easy way for the landlord to say yes. If the user has not given their current rent, the proposed rent, or comparable prices, ask for them or leave clearly labelled brackets.

**Structure (all four, in order):** thanks for notice → counter with a specific number → one or two reasons that are about facts, not your budget (comparable listings, years in the unit, payment record, condition of the flat) → a concrete ask (sign for 12 months at that number, or a call this week).

**Before sending:** check whether the lease allows an increase before renewal, and ask for any change in writing.

> Hi Mark,
> Thanks for letting me know about the proposed increase to $2,100. I'd like to ask you to reconsider. Comparable units in the building are listing at $1,850 to $1,950, and I've been here three years with rent always on time. I'd be glad to sign another 12 months at $1,900. Would that work for you? Happy to talk it through by phone if easier.
> Best,
> Jess

**If they hold firm:**
> Understood. Could you meet me halfway at $2,000, with the bathroom fan repaired before the new term starts? I'd sign today on that basis.

## Repair request (and follow-up)

**First:**
> Hi Mark, the kitchen tap has been leaking since Monday and it's getting worse; there's water pooling under the sink. Could you arrange a plumber this week? I'm home after 5pm any day, or can leave a key. Photos attached.

**Reminder, one week later:**
> Following up on the tap leak from the 12th. It's now damaging the cabinet base. Could you confirm a repair date by Friday? If it's easier, I can book a plumber and deduct it from rent with your written okay.

**Formal, after two weeks:**
> This is a formal repair request for the kitchen tap leak first reported on 12 May. Under the lease, repairs affecting water and fixtures are the landlord's responsibility. Please confirm a repair date within seven days. I'd much prefer to sort this informally, so do call me.

## Getting a deposit back

> Hi Mark, I moved out on the 30th and left the flat clean with the keys as agreed; photos from the day attached. Could you confirm when the $1,800 deposit will be returned? Under the lease it's due within 14 days. Thanks for the last three years, it was a good place to live.

**If they deduct unfairly:**
> Thanks for the breakdown. I don't agree with the $400 for repainting: the walls were in the same condition as at move-in, per the photos in the inventory and mine attached. Please return that portion. I'm happy to accept the $80 cleaning charge. If we can't agree, I'll raise it with the deposit scheme, but I'd rather settle it directly.

## Contractor: quote too high / work not finished / overcharged

**Quote too high:**
> Thanks for the quote. It's above what I'd budgeted. Is there a way to bring it under $3,000, maybe by dropping [item] or using [alternative]? I'd like to work with you on this.

**Work not finished:**
> Hi Dave, the job was due to finish on the 20th and the tiling in the bathroom is still incomplete. Could you give me a date this week for completion? I'll release the final payment once it's done as agreed.

**Overcharged:**
> The invoice came in at $1,450 against the agreed $1,100. Could you walk me through the difference? If there was extra work I wasn't told about, I'd like to understand it before paying. Happy to pay the agreed $1,100 today.

## Landlord to tenant (the other side)

**Rent increase notice, kind version:**
> Hi Jess, I'm writing to give notice that rent will increase to $2,000 from 1 September, in line with the market and the higher building costs this year. You've been a great tenant and I'd like you to stay. If you'd like to talk it through, I'm around.

**Late rent, first message:**
> Hi Jess, rent for May hasn't come through yet. Could you check on it? If something's changed on your side, let me know and we can work something out.

## Avoid
- "This is unacceptable."
- Threatening legal action in the first message.
- Long emotional histories.
- Withholding rent without written agreement or legal advice.
- Only calling. If it isn't written, it didn't happen.
