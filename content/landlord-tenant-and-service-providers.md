# Landlords, tenants, contractors and service providers

## Goal
Get the outcome and keep the relationship workable, because you will need this person again. Written, dated, calm.

## Principles
- Written, always. If you call, follow up with "confirming what we discussed".
- Facts, dates, amounts. Feelings weaken these messages.
- Ask for a specific thing by a specific date.
- Reference the agreement or the law once, neutrally, not as a threat. "Under the lease" is enough.
- Stay pleasant and precise; that person wins most disputes.

## Rent increase, pushing back

**Do not send a flat no**; it usually ends in non-renewal. Counter with a specific number, one or two strong reasons and an easy yes. If the user has not given the current rent, the proposed rent or comparable prices, ask or leave labelled brackets.

**Structure (all four, in order):** thanks for notice → counter with a specific number → one or two reasons that are about facts, not your budget (comparable listings, years in the unit, payment record, condition of the flat) → a concrete ask (sign for 12 months at that number, or a call this week).

**Before sending:** check whether the lease allows an increase before renewal.

> Hi Mark,
> Thanks for letting me know about the increase to $2,100. I'd like to ask you to reconsider. Comparable units in the building list at $1,850 to $1,950, and I've paid on time for three years. I'd be glad to sign another 12 months at $1,900. Would that work? Happy to talk by phone.
> Best,
> Jess

**If they hold firm:**
> Understood. Could you meet me halfway at $2,000, with the bathroom fan repaired before the new term starts? I'd sign today on that basis.

## Repair request (and follow-up)

**First:**
> Hi Mark, the kitchen tap has leaked since Monday and water is pooling under the sink. Could you arrange a plumber this week? I'm home after 5pm, or can leave a key. Photos attached.

**Reminder, one week later:**
> Following up on the tap leak from the 12th; it's now damaging the cabinet. Could you confirm a repair date by Friday? Or I can book a plumber and deduct it from rent, with your written okay.

**Formal, after two weeks:**
> This is a formal repair request for the kitchen tap leak first reported on 12 May. Under the lease, water and fixture repairs are the landlord's responsibility. Please confirm a repair date within seven days. I'd still prefer to sort this informally, so do call me.

## Getting a deposit back

> Hi Mark, I moved out on the 30th and left the flat clean with the keys as agreed; photos attached. Could you confirm when the $1,800 deposit will be returned? Under the lease it's due within 14 days. Thanks for three good years.

**If they deduct unfairly:**
> Thanks for the breakdown. I don't agree with the $400 for repainting: the walls were in the same condition as at move-in, per the photos in the inventory and mine attached. Please return that portion. I'm happy to accept the $80 cleaning charge. If we can't agree, I'll raise it with the deposit scheme, but I'd rather settle it directly.

## Contractor: quote too high / work not finished / overcharged

**Quote too high:**
> Thanks for the quote. It's above my budget. Could we get it under $3,000, maybe by dropping the heated floor or using standard tiles? I'd like to work with you on this.

**Work not finished:**
> Hi Dave, the job was due to finish on the 20th and the bathroom tiling is still incomplete. Could you give me a completion date this week? I'll release the final payment once it's done, as agreed.

**Overcharged:**
> The invoice is $1,450 against the agreed $1,100. Could you walk me through the difference before I pay it? Happy to pay the agreed $1,100 today.

## Landlord to tenant (the other side)

**Rent increase notice, kind version:**
> Hi Jess, rent will increase to $2,000 from 1 September, in line with the market and this year's higher building costs. You've been a great tenant and I'd like you to stay. Happy to talk it through.

**Late rent, first message:**
> Hi Jess, rent for May hasn't come through yet. Could you check on it? If something's changed on your side, let me know and we can work something out.

## Avoid
- "This is unacceptable."
- Threatening legal action in the first message.
- Withholding rent without written agreement or legal advice.
- Only calling. If it isn't written, it didn't happen.

## If they refuse, ignore you or get hostile
Move one step up the ladder (request, dated reminder, formal notice), never two. Reply in writing even if they call. If they are rude, answer only the facts. If they refuse outright, say calmly what you will do next (deposit scheme, tenancy board, small claims), then do it. Never threaten a step you would not take.
> I'll treat the 14 days as passed on Friday. If the deposit hasn't been returned by then, I'll open a claim with the deposit scheme, which I'd rather avoid. Happy to talk before then.
> I'll leave the tone to one side. The leak was reported on 12 May and is still unrepaired. Please confirm a date this week.
