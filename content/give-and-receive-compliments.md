# Compliments and appreciation

## Giving a compliment

**The rule:** specific + effect on you. "The way you handled that client call was so calm, I was taking notes" beats "you're great at your job."

- Compliment effort and choices over innate traits. "You clearly put real thought into the menu" is better than "you're so talented."
- Do not attach an ask.
- Do not qualify it ("I don't usually say this but").
- To someone senior: keep it short and about a specific thing. Gushing reads as sucking up.
- Written compliments are kept. Send the text.

**Examples**
> Your toast last night was the best I've heard at a wedding, it actually sounded like her.
> The way you explained that in the meeting made something click that I'd been stuck on for weeks. Thank you.
> I've noticed you always make new people feel welcome. It matters more than you probably think.

## Receiving a compliment

**The rule:** "Thank you" and a short sentence that accepts it. Do not deflect, minimise, or bounce it back immediately.

- Deflecting ("oh it was nothing") rejects their judgement.
- Immediate bounce ("no, you're the one who's great") makes it a transaction.
- If you want to credit others, do it after accepting: "Thank you. Priya did half of it, honestly, but I'm proud of it."

**Examples**
> Thank you, that means a lot coming from you.
> Thanks, I worked hard on that bit, so I'm glad it showed.
> That's really kind, thank you. I'm still learning but I'm getting there.

## Writing genuine appreciation (thank-you message)

**Structure:** what they did (specific) + what it meant / what it made possible + one line about them.

**Examples**

**After someone helped you move:**
> Thank you for Saturday. You gave up a whole day and carried the worst of the boxes without a single complaint. I'd have been at it till midnight without you. Dinner's on me next week, no arguments.

**Mentor or someone senior:**
> I wanted to say thank you for the intro to Nadia. The conversation was exactly what I needed and I wouldn't have got in the room without you. I'll let you know how it goes.

**Thank-you after a hard time:**
> I don't think I said it properly at the time, but you showing up every week when Dad was sick is something I'll never forget.

**Quick, casual:**
> You're a legend, thank you.

## Avoid
- "You're so lucky to be good at that" (erases effort).
- Compliment + request in the same breath.
- "No, you're amazing!" ping-pong.
- Thanking so profusely they have to manage your gratitude.
