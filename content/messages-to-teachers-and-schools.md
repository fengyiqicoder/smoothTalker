# Messages to teachers and schools

## Goal
The teacher reads you as a partner, not an opponent, and something specific happens next: a meeting, a plan, a change. Teachers get many messages from anxious parents; the ones that work are short, specific and assume good intent.

## Structure
1. **Who you are**: your name and your child's name and class.
2. **What you have noticed, as facts**: what your child said or did, when, how often.
3. **A question or a request**, not a verdict. "Have you noticed anything similar?" "Could we meet?"
4. **What you are doing at home**, if relevant. It shows you are working together.
5. **Thanks, and your availability.**

## Principles
- Start with the class teacher. Going straight to the head or the governors usually slows things down and costs goodwill.
- Your child's account is real to them and partial. Ask what the teacher saw before concluding.
- One concern per message. A list of grievances gets triaged; one clear concern gets action.
- Write in office hours and expect a reply in a day or two. Do not message a teacher's personal phone or social accounts.
- For bullying, safety or wellbeing, be specific about dates and what happened, and ask what the school's process is. These deserve a prompt reply, and it is fair to say so.
- Keep a copy of what you send. If you need to escalate, you will want the dates.
- Assume the teacher cares about your child. Most do, and the message lands better when you write as if they do.

## Examples

**Raising a worry about a child's wellbeing:**
> Hi Ms Patel, I'm Leo's mum (Year 4). Leo has come home upset three times this week, saying some boys at lunch won't let him sit with them and call him names. I don't know the full picture, so I wanted to ask whether you've noticed anything. Could we have a quick chat this week? I'm free after 3:15 any day. Thank you.

**Asking for a meeting about progress:**
> Hello Mr Owens, I'd like to understand how Maya is getting on in maths. She's been finding the homework hard and getting upset about it. Could we find 15 minutes in the next week or two? I'm happy to do a call if that's easier.

**Absence note:**
> Hi, Aisha (7B) will be off today and tomorrow with a stomach bug. We expect her back on Thursday. Could she pick up any work she misses? Thanks, Sarah Khan

**Questioning a grade, collaboratively:**
> Hi Dr Lin, Jamie showed me the feedback on his history essay (C). He was surprised, since he spent a lot of time on it. Could you tell me what the main gap was, so we can work on it at home? Thanks for your help.

**Homework load:**
> Hi Mrs Garcia, a quick note that Ben has been spending over two hours a night on homework, often until 9:30, and he's getting quite tired. Is that what you'd expect for Year 6? If not, I wonder if something is taking him longer than it should.

**Disagreeing with a decision (a detention, a placement):**
> Thanks for explaining the detention. I've talked with Ruby, and I understand the rule. I do think the circumstances were unusual, since she was helping a friend who felt unwell. Could we talk briefly about it? I'm not asking you to overturn it, I'd just like us to be on the same page.

**Escalating after no response or no change (to the head of year):**
> Dear Mr Ahmed, I'm writing about the lunchtime bullying Leo has experienced since 3 October. I raised it with Ms Patel on 5 and 12 October (copies below) and it has continued. Could you let me know what the school's next steps are, and when we could meet?

## Avoid
- Opening with "I'm very disappointed" or "I demand".
- Copying the head into the first message.
- Criticising the teacher to your child, or quoting them to other parents.
- Long histories. Dates and facts, then the ask.
- Messaging at 11pm and chasing at 7am.
- Discussing other children by name, beyond what is needed to describe what happened.

## If it goes badly
If the teacher is defensive, go back to the shared goal: your child doing well in their class. If there is no reply after three school days, send one short follow-up; after that, go to the head of year or the school office, with dates. If a formal decision still seems wrong, ask for the school's complaints policy and follow it in writing. If you believe your child is unsafe, contact the school's safeguarding lead directly and say so in the first line.
> I think we both want Leo to feel settled at lunch. What would you suggest we try first?
> I haven't heard back about my message on the 5th, so I'm following up. Could you let me know when we might talk?
