# Requesting an introduction or referral

## Goal
Your contact can forward one message and be done. The ask costs them almost nothing and puts their reputation at minimal risk.

## Structure
1. **The ask, in the first line.** Who, and why them.
2. **Why it's a reasonable intro** (what you offer the other person, not just what you want).
3. **A forwardable blurb**, separate paragraph, written in third person or as a self-introduction they can paste.
4. **A real out.** They may not be close enough, or may not want to spend the capital.
5. **Promise to report back.**

## Principles
- Double opt-in: the best intros ask the other person first. Make it easy for your contact to do that by giving them the blurb.
- Never ask for an intro to someone you could just email yourself. Use intros for people who would otherwise ignore you.
- Be specific about the ask you'll make of the other person. "A 20-minute call about X" not "pick their brain".
- Report back. People stop making intros for those who vanish afterward.
- For referrals (jobs), make it explicit they can say no without explanation; their name is on the line.

## Examples

**Intro request, email or DM:**
> Hi Jo,
> Could I ask a favour? I'd love an intro to Dana Park at Helix. I'm building a tool for exactly the onboarding problem she wrote about last month and I think a 20-minute conversation would be useful for both of us. If you're not close enough for that to be comfortable, no worries at all, just say.
>
> If you're up for it, here's a blurb you can forward:
>
> "Dana, I'd like to introduce Sam Lee. Sam runs Northstar, which builds guest-checkout tools for e-commerce, and has some interesting data on the onboarding drop-off you posted about. Sam's asked for 20 minutes to compare notes; happy to connect you if you're open to it."
>
> I'll let you know how it goes either way. Thanks, Jo.
> Sam

**Job referral, to a friend at the company:**
> Hey, I'm applying for the senior designer role at your place. Would you be comfortable referring me? Totally understand if you'd rather not put your name on it; we've never worked together directly, so I won't take it personally. If yes, I'll send you the job link and a two-line summary to make it painless.

**Asking a former manager for a reference:**
> Hi Priya, I'm in the final round for a role at [company] and they've asked for references. Would you be willing to be one? They'd call for about 15 minutes, sometime next week. If you're not able to, no problem, I'd just appreciate a quick heads-up so I can ask someone else.

**Reporting back (do not skip this):**
> Quick update: Dana and I spoke on Tuesday, really useful, and she's introduced me to their platform lead. Thank you for making that happen; I owe you a coffee.

## Avoid
- "Do you know anyone who could help me with...?" (vague, makes them do the thinking).
- Asking for multiple intros at once.
- Not providing a blurb.
- Following up on the intro with your contact more than once.
- Asking for an intro and then not using it.
- Forgetting to close the loop.
