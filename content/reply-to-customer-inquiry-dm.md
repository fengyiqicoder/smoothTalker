# Replying to a customer inquiry in DMs

## Goal
Answer the question in the first line, then move them one step closer to buying or booking, without pressure. DMs are conversations, not tickets.

## Structure
1. **Answer first.** Price, availability, yes/no. Do not make them ask twice.
2. **One useful extra** they did not ask but will want (sizes, delivery time, what's included).
3. **The next step**, specific and easy: a link, a question that moves toward a decision, an offer to hold it.
4. **Their name if you have it, no sign-off.**

## Principles
- Speed is the biggest lever in DMs. A fast "yes, $45, here's the link" beats a perfect reply an hour later.
- Match their energy. If they wrote two words, don't write two paragraphs.
- Never "DM for price" style evasions. Give the number.
- Ask one question, not three. Each question is a hurdle.
- If they go quiet after a price, one gentle follow-up in 24 to 48 hours, then stop.
- Sound like a person. No "Thank you for reaching out to us!"

## Examples

**"How much is this?"**
> Hi Jess! That one's $45, and it comes in the green and the oat colour. Free shipping over $60. Want me to send the link, or hold one for you?

**"Is this still available?"**
> Yes, still here! It's the last one in medium. I can hold it till tomorrow evening if you want; just say the word.

**"Do you do custom orders?"**
> We do! Custom pieces start at $120 and take about two weeks. Tell me roughly what you're thinking and I'll give you a proper quote.

**"Can you tell me more about the workshop?"**
> Of course. It's Saturday the 14th, 2 to 5pm, at the studio in Peckham. $80 includes all materials and you leave with a finished piece. Beginner-friendly, max eight people. There are three spots left; want me to save you one?

**Service business, "are you free next week?"**
> I've got Tuesday 10am or Thursday 2pm free next week. Either work? If not, send me a couple of times and I'll make one fit.

**Follow-up after silence (once):**
> Hey Jess, just checking whether you'd like me to keep holding the green one; totally fine if you've gone off it.

**They ask for a discount:**
> I don't discount individual pieces, but if you grab two the shipping's free, and there's a 10% code for newsletter sign-ups. Want it?

**Question you can't answer immediately:**
> Good question, let me check with the maker and come back to you by tonight.

## Avoid
- "Thanks for reaching out! Please visit our website for more information."
- Copy-paste templates that ignore their actual question.
- Three questions in one reply.
- Emoji overload.
- Hard-sell pressure ("only 2 left, buy now!!").
- Leaving inquiries unanswered for a day.
