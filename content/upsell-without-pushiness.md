# Suggesting more without being pushy

## Goal
The customer feels advised, not sold to. Even if they say no, they trust you more.

## The rule
Only suggest something that is genuinely better for them given what they've already told you. If you cannot say why it is better for them in one sentence, do not suggest it.

## Structure
1. **Confirm what they're getting** (so they feel handled).
2. **The suggestion, framed around their situation.** "Since you mentioned X..."
3. **One honest trade-off** (price, time). Honesty about the cost is what makes it advice.
4. **An explicit easy no.**

## Principles
- One suggestion. A menu of add-ons feels like a checkout page.
- Say when the cheaper option is fine. "Honestly, for what you described, the basic one is enough" builds the trust that sells the next thing.
- Timing: after they've decided to buy, or after they've had a good experience. Not before the first yes.
- Never manufacture urgency. Real deadlines are fine; fake ones are noticed.
- For repeat purchases, a reminder with a reason beats a discount.

## Examples

**Add-on at point of sale, DM:**
> Great, the medium tote it is. One thought since you said it's for a laptop: the padded insert is $18 and makes it a lot safer for daily commuting. Not essential, just say if you want it added.

**Suggesting the bigger package, service:**
> Based on what you've told me about the two-bathroom flat, the standard clean will work. If you want the inside of the oven and windows done too, that's the deep clean at $40 more; most people do that one first time and then standard after. Either's fine.

**Honest downsell (this is what makes upsells work later):**
> For a one-off event you don't need the pro plan; the basic one covers everything you listed. If you end up running these monthly, that's when the pro plan pays for itself.

**Repeat purchase nudge:**
> Hey Sam, it's been about three months since your last bag of the Ethiopian, which is roughly when people run out. Want me to send another? Same as before, or try the new Colombian?

**Suggesting a related product after a good experience:**
> So glad the serum's working for you. If you want to build on it, the moisturiser is the thing people pair with it most, but it's not needed; the serum does the heavy lifting on its own.

**Premium tier, when they ask "what's the difference":**
> The premium is $30 more and the real difference is [one concrete thing]. If [their situation], it's worth it. If not, the standard is genuinely fine and I'd rather you get what you'll use.

**Client, scope expansion:**
> Now that the site's live, the natural next step is the email flows; that's usually where the next revenue jump comes from. Happy to scope it if it's useful, or we can leave it here.

## Avoid
- "Would you like to add...?" scripts.
- "Customers who bought this also bought."
- Fake scarcity.
- Recommending the expensive one to everyone.
- Multiple add-ons at once.
- Following up on a declined suggestion.
