# Roommates and shared living

## Goal
The problem gets fixed and you can still make toast in the same kitchen tomorrow. You cannot walk away from a roommate after a bad message, so every message is written for the next six months, not the next ten minutes.

## Structure
1. **Friendly, low-key opener.** Match how you normally talk in the house.
2. **The specific issue, as a fact about the house**, not a verdict on the person. "The bins went out late three weeks running" beats "you never do the bins."
3. **A concrete proposal** (a rota, a split, a quiet-hours window, a guest rule). Arriving with a fix turns a complaint into a decision.
4. **An easy way to adjust it.** "Happy to tweak it if another setup works better."
5. **Keep it short.** Long messages in a shared house get screenshotted and discussed without you.

## Principles
- Raise it early and small. Resentment that sits for two months comes out as a paragraph nobody deserves.
- Speak for the house when it is a house problem, and for yourself when it is yours. Do not claim "everyone's annoyed" unless everyone agreed you could say so.
- Money and chores go in writing (the house chat or a shared note). Feelings go in person or on a call.
- Propose systems, not personalities. A rota, a shared bills app, or a quiet-hours rule does the nagging so you do not have to.
- One issue per conversation. Stacking the dishes, the guest and the heating bill into one message reads as a case file.
- Assume they have not noticed. Most roommate problems are about different baselines, not bad intentions.

## Examples

**Chores, proposing a rota in the house chat:**
> Hey all, the kitchen's been getting away from us lately (me included). Want to try a simple weekly rota? I've made a draft: kitchen, bathroom, bins, hoovering, swap each Monday. Shout if you'd change anything, otherwise let's start this week.

**One person not doing their share, privately:**
> Hey, can I raise something small? The dishes have been piling up a few days at a time and I've ended up doing most of them. Could we agree nothing stays in the sink overnight? Happy to do the same.

**Bills, a late payment:**
> Hi Sam, the electricity bill came out on the 3rd, your share is £46. Can you send it over by Friday? I've set up a split in Splitwise so it's all tracked from now on.

**A partner who has effectively moved in:**
> I really like Jo, and I get why they're here a lot. It's been 4 or 5 nights a week for a while now, which changes the bills and the bathroom queue. Can we either keep it to a couple of nights, or talk about Jo chipping in for utilities?

**Noise, late at night:**
> Hey, could we keep music and calls down after 11 on weeknights? I'm up at 6 for work and the walls carry more than you'd think. Weekends I'm not fussed.

**Giving notice that you're moving out:**
> Heads up, I've decided to move out at the end of March. I'll give the landlord proper notice this week and help find someone for my room if you'd like a say in who. Thanks for making this a good place to live.

**Ending on good terms, deposit and handover:**
> Before I go, can we do a quick walk-round on the 28th so we agree what state my room and the shared bits are in? I'll deep clean the bathroom and settle my share of the last bills by then.

## Avoid
- Passive-aggressive notes on the fridge or in the kitchen.
- "Some people in this house..." in the group chat. Everyone knows who you mean, including them.
- Raising it at 1am when you are woken up. Say "could you turn it down, I'm sleeping" then, and have the real conversation tomorrow.
- Keeping a tally of every wrong and producing it all at once.
- Speaking for other roommates who have not agreed to it.
- Threatening to tell the landlord as an opening move.

## If it goes badly
If they get defensive ("I do loads, you just don't see it"), do not argue the tally. Agree the goal and go back to the system: "Fair, let's just put it on a rota so we both can see it." If a money issue drags past two reminders, move it to a written record and say plainly when the bill is due, because this is the item that can affect your deposit and credit. If nothing changes after a real conversation, check your tenancy: a joint tenancy may make you liable for their share, so contact the landlord or agent early and in writing, then decide whether it is you or them who moves.
> Fair enough, I don't want to get into who did what. Can we just try the rota for a month and see?
> I've asked a couple of times about the £46. I need it by the 15th, or I'll talk to the agent about how the bills are set up.
