# Saying no to a customer request without losing the customer

## Goal
The customer gets a clear answer, understands it is not personal, and leaves with something: an alternative, a timeline, or a real explanation. A clean no keeps more customers than a vague maybe.

## Structure
1. **Thank them for the specific ask.** One line, no gush.
2. **The no, plainly, in the first two sentences.** "We can't do a custom size on this one."
3. **The real reason in one line.** Cost, quality, capacity, fairness to other customers. Not "policy".
4. **What you can do.** An alternative, a partial, a future date, or a referral to someone who does it.
5. **Leave the door open** without promising: "If that changes, I'll tell you."

## Principles
- Say "can't" or "don't", not "unable to at this time". Vague nos invite a second ask.
- "Policy" is not a reason. Say what the policy protects: "we close returns at 30 days because after that we can't resell the item".
- Offer what you can before they ask. Reactive concessions feel extracted; proactive ones feel generous.
- If you bend the rule, decide fast and say it is an exception, so it does not become the baseline.
- Bend for good reasons: a long-standing customer, your own mistake, a true edge case. Do not bend for volume of complaint. Rewarding pressure buys more pressure.
- Tickets can run five sentences. DMs two or three.
- Never say a feature is "on the roadmap" unless it is.

## Examples

**Feature request, support ticket:**
> Hi Lena, thanks for the detailed suggestion about bulk export to CSV. We're not building it this year; the team is focused on the mobile app, and I'd rather tell you that than leave you waiting. What works today: the API can pull the same data (docs here [link]), and I can send a short script that does the export if that helps. I've logged your request against the feature so you'll get an email if it ships.
> Omar

**Custom order, DM:**
> Thanks for asking! I don't do custom sizes on the ceramic planters, the kiln setup makes one-offs cost more than anyone would pay. The closest I have is the 22cm, which fits most windowsills. Send me a photo of the spot and I'll tell you honestly whether it'll work.

**Rush job, email:**
> Hi Dan, I can't turn this around by Friday. Done properly it takes six working days and I have two projects ahead of you. What I can do: deliver on Wednesday the 18th, or, if Friday is fixed, the first section by Friday and the rest the following week. Which is more useful?
> Ruth

**Expired discount code:**
> Hi Sam, that code ended on 30 June, so it won't apply to this order. The SUMMER10 code is live until the 15th and takes 10% off, which is close to the same. Here's the link with it applied [link]. If you already paid full price, reply with the order number and I'll refund the difference.

**Return outside the window:**
> Hi Jess, the return window is 30 days and this was delivered 47 days ago, so I can't take it back for a refund. I can offer store credit for the full amount, valid for a year, or 30% off a replacement if the fit was the problem. Which would you prefer?

**Return outside the window, exception granted:**
> Hi Jess, the window is 30 days and this is at 47, so normally this would be a no. Since you've ordered six times and never returned anything, I'll make an exception this once and refund in full. Label attached. Going forward the 30 days is firm, so there's no surprise next time.

**Request that violates policy (a second user on a personal plan):**
> Thanks for asking rather than just doing it. I can't add a second login to a personal plan; it's one seat, and the team plan exists exactly for this. Team for two is $18 a month, $6 more than now, and I can apply the upgrade with the rest of this month free. Want me to do that?

**Something you simply do not do, DM:**
> I don't do wholesale, it's just me and a small kiln! For larger quantities, [maker] does similar work at volume and I'd happily introduce you.

## Avoid
- "Unfortunately" and "I'm afraid" as openers.
- "Our policy states."
- A soft maybe that leaves them waiting for a yes.
- Apologising more than once.
- Granting an exception without saying it is one.
- "On the roadmap" when it is not.

## If they push back
Restate the no in one line, repeat the alternative, and add no new reasons. "I understand, and the answer's still that I can't refund it. The store credit is yours whenever you want it." If they threaten a bad review, do not change the answer because of the threat: "That's your call. I'd still like to sort this with the credit or the exchange, and either is ready when you are." If they bring a genuinely new fact (the item was faulty, your site gave wrong information), reconsider on that fact and say so: "That changes it. If the size guide said 42, I'll take it back."
