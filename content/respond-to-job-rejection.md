# Responding to a job or pitch rejection

## Goal
The person who rejected you finishes reading your reply thinking "I hope we get to work with them later". You get one useful piece of information if it exists, and you leave with your dignity and their number.

## Structure
1. **Thank them for the specific thing**, not "the opportunity". The time, the depth of the process, a conversation you actually enjoyed.
2. **Accept the decision in one line.** No re-litigating, no "I'm surprised".
3. **One small ask, optional.** Feedback, or to be kept in mind. Never both in the same message.
4. **Make the ask easy to answer**: a narrow question, a stated time budget, and permission to skip it.
5. **Close warm and short.** Name a future you would welcome.

## Principles
- Reply within a day. A rejection reply that arrives fast reads as composed; one that arrives a week later reads as brooding.
- The single way to get real feedback is to ask a narrow question and make it cheap to answer. "Any feedback?" gets a template. "Was there one thing that would have changed the outcome?" gets a sentence, and a sentence is what you need.
- Ask for feedback from the person you spoke with most, not the recruiter, unless the recruiter is all you have.
- Hiring decisions are mostly about the other candidate, the budget, or timing. Take feedback as data, not verdict.
- Bitterness leaks. If you cannot write it warm yet, wait a day, then write it.
- Being kept in mind is real. Around one in ten hires at small companies comes from a previous runner-up. Make that easy by staying friendly and visible.

## Examples

**Gracious close that keeps the door open:**
> Hi Priya, thanks for letting me know, and for how open the whole process was. I enjoyed the conversation with Tom about the migration, and I'd be glad to talk again if a similar role opens up. Good luck with the launch.
> Sam

**Asking for feedback after rejection (the version that gets answered):**
> Thanks for the update, and no hard feelings. One quick question, only if it takes you under a minute: was there one thing that would have tipped it the other way? A single line is plenty, and it's fine to say you can't share. Either way, thank you for your time.

**"We went with someone else" after a long process (four rounds, take-home task):**
> Thanks for telling me directly. Four rounds plus the task was a real investment on both sides, so I'd value one specific thing from the panel, if you're able to share it. And I'd genuinely like to be considered for the next opening on the team. Congratulations on the hire.

**Rejected by a client after a pitch:**
> Thanks for the decision, Marcus, and for the clear brief throughout. If you're able, I'd like to know whether it came down to price, approach or fit, so I pitch better next time. One word is enough. If the project needs a second pair of hands later, I'm around.

**Recruiter says the role was closed or paused:**
> Understood, thanks for the heads-up. Would you keep me on your list for anything similar in [area], ideally in the next quarter? Happy to send an updated CV whenever useful.

**Short reply to a template rejection (no relationship to protect):**
> Thanks for letting me know. Best of luck with the search.

**Responding to feedback that stings but is fair:**
> That's useful, thank you for being straight with me. I'll work on [the specific point]. If the team is hiring again in six months, I'd like another shot.

## Avoid
- Asking why. "Why" makes people defensive; "what one thing" makes them helpful.
- "I'm surprised, I thought the interview went well." True or not, it sounds like a complaint.
- Replying to all, or to the CEO, when the rejection came from a recruiter.
- Explaining your circumstances, your other offers, or how much you wanted it.
- Sending a rewritten application or a "what if I did it at a lower salary" counter.
- Naming the person you assume they hired.

## If they ghost instead of rejecting
Two touches, then let it go. After the date they gave you, wait three working days.
> Hi Priya, checking in on the [role] decision, since you'd mentioned hearing back by the 20th. If it's a no, a one-line reply is completely fine and I'd appreciate the closure.
One week later, if still nothing:
> I'll take silence as a no and close this on my end. Thanks for the conversations, and I'm open to talking again if things change.
Then stop. Do not chase a third time. Log it, move on, and if they come back months later, you can still say yes.
