# First message to a stranger

## Goal
They reply. That requires: it is clearly about them, it is easy to answer, and it does not ask for much.

## Structure
1. **Proof you looked**: reference one specific thing from their profile, post, work.
2. **A reaction or question about that thing.** Open questions that are easy to answer.
3. **Nothing about you** beyond a clause, unless it connects to their thing.
4. **No ask** in the first message (dating), or a tiny, clear ask (professional).

## Principles
- "Hey" and "how's it going" put all the work on them. Give them something to grab.
- Specific beats clever. A genuinely curious question about their thing beats a joke.
- Short. Two to four sentences. A wall of text to a stranger is a red flag.
- Compliments on appearance (dating) or achievements (professional) are the least interesting thing you can say; everyone says them.
- One message. If no reply, one follow-up at most a week later, then stop.

## Examples

**Dating app, their profile mentions hiking in Scotland:**
> Skye or the Cairngorms? I've only done one and I'm being told I picked wrong.

**Dating app, a photo of them cooking:**
> Okay that pasta looks better than any restaurant I've been to this year. Is it a recipe or a "watched my nan" situation?

**Dating, after a match with a blank-ish profile:**
> Your profile is mysterious, so I'll go first: I'm the kind of person who reads restaurant menus in advance. What's your equivalent?

**Professional cold DM / email to someone you admire:**
> Hi Rosa, your talk on onboarding flows at Config changed how we run our first-week emails; the "delay the tour" idea alone moved our activation rate. I'm building something similar for a nonprofit and had one specific question about [X]. Would you be open to a two-line reply? Totally understand if not.

**Networking, someone at a company you're interested in:**
> Hi Dev, I saw you moved to [company] last year. I'm looking at a similar move and would love 15 minutes of your honest take on the culture, at a time that suits you. Happy to send questions in advance so it's quick.

**Community (forum, Discord, hobby group):**
> Your write-up on [thing] was the first one that actually made [concept] click for me. Quick question if you have a sec: [specific].

**Someone you saw at an event:**
> Hi, we didn't get to talk at [event] but I caught your question about [X] and thought it was the sharpest one in the room. Would be up for a coffee if you're ever around [area].

## Avoid
- "Hey" / "Hi" / "How are you" alone.
- Generic compliments.
- Copy-paste openers (people can tell).
- Asking for a call in a first professional message without offering to make it easy.
- Explaining who you are for three sentences.
- Asking two questions at once.
- Negging, "jokes" at their expense, or anything that would be weird said aloud in a coffee shop.
