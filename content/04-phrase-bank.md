# Phrase bank

Pick one phrase per function at most. A message built entirely of phrase-bank lines sounds assembled. Adapt to the user's voice.

## Openers that create context fast
- "Following up on [specific thing] from [when]."
- "Quick one about [topic]."  (only if it is actually quick)
- "I've been thinking about what you said about [X]."
- "Two things: [A] and [B]."  (for multi-point messages, tell them the count)
- "Before I forget:"  (casual, low stakes)
- "I owe you a reply on [X], sorry for the wait."  (one apology, then content)

## Acknowledgement (before redirecting or disagreeing)
- "That makes sense, and..."  (not "but")
- "I can see why you'd read it that way."
- "You're right that [specific true thing]."
- "I hadn't thought about [X], that's fair."
- "I know this isn't the answer you were hoping for."
- "I get that this is urgent on your side."

## Softeners that keep the point intact
- "My honest read is..."
- "I'd push back a little on..."
- "Here's my hesitation:"
- "Can I offer a different take?"
- "I might be wrong on this, but..."  (use once, only when actually uncertain)
- "Here's what I can do:"  (after a no)

## Making an ask
- "Could you [specific action] by [specific time]?"
- "Would you be up for [X]?"
- "What would it take to [X]?"  (when they hold the leverage)
- "Is there a version of this that works for you?"
- "I'd really value your read on [X]."  (asking for input, not labour)
- "Can I get 15 minutes this week?"  (name the cost)

## Giving an out
- "If this week is packed, next week is fine."
- "Totally fine to say no."  (once)
- "If it's easier, I can [alternative]."
- "No need to reply if you're good with it."
- "Whichever is easier for you."

## Declining
- "I'm going to pass on this one."
- "I can't take this on right now."
- "That's not something I can do, but [alternative]."
- "I'm at capacity through [date]."
- "I'd rather be upfront than let you down later:"
- "Not this time, but please keep me in mind."

## Bridges to next steps
- "So the next step on my side is [X]; on yours, [Y]."
- "I'll send [X] by [when] and we can go from there."
- "Let's lock a time: [two options]?"
- "Leaving this with you; no rush."

## Closers
- Text: nothing, or "talk soon", or their emoji habit
- Warm email: "Thanks again," / "Appreciate you," / "Talk soon,"
- Neutral email: "Best," / "Thanks,"
- After a boundary or a no: "Thanks for understanding," (neutral, not "Hope that's okay!!")
- After bad news: "I'm around if you want to talk it through."
- After an apology: "Thanks for your patience," (not another sorry)

## Words that quietly improve a message
- "and" instead of "but" after an acknowledgement
- "when" instead of "if" for things that will happen ("when you get a chance")
- "I" statements for feelings, "we" statements for plans, "you" statements only for appreciation
- The person's name once, early
- A specific date instead of "soon", "later", "sometime"
