# Introducing yourself

## Goal
People remember one thing about you and feel invited to reach out. Not a CV. Not a performance.

## Structure
1. **Name and role/reason you're here**, one line.
2. **One concrete thing** about what you do or care about (not "passionate about innovation").
3. **One human thing** that gives people a hook (a hobby, where you're from, an odd fact).
4. **An invitation**: what you'd like to hear from them, or how to reach you.

## Principles
- Under 100 words for written intros. Thirty seconds spoken.
- Specific beats impressive. "I spent last year fixing our checkout flow" beats "I'm a results-driven product leader".
- The human thing is the part people remember. Pick one that invites conversation.
- Say what you want. "I'd love to hear what's annoying you about the current system" makes people talk to you.
- Match the room. A Slack intro is casual; a board intro is not.

## Examples

**New job, Slack or Teams:**
> Hi all, I'm Maya, joining the platform team as an engineer today. Most recently I was at [company] working on payments infrastructure, so if anything payment-shaped is broken, I'm your person. Outside work I'm slowly restoring a 1970s bike and mostly failing. I'd love 15 minutes with anyone who wants to tell me what's annoying about the current system; DMs open.

**First day, spoken to the team:**
> Hi, I'm Maya. I've come from [company] where I spent three years on payments. I'm here to help with the platform rebuild. I'll spend the first couple of weeks mostly listening, so grab me if there's something you think I should know. I'm also new to the city, so restaurant recommendations welcome.

**Online community / forum / Discord:**
> Hey, I'm Sam, a self-taught designer working mostly on tiny apps for local businesses. I joined because I keep getting stuck on onboarding flows and this seems like the place people actually talk about it. I'll mostly lurk to start; if you have a favourite thread on onboarding, point me at it.

**Networking event, spoken:**
> I'm Sam, I do design for small shops, mostly the boring-but-important bits like checkout. What are you working on?

**New to a parents' group, sports club, neighbourhood chat:**
> Hi everyone, I'm Jo, we moved into number 14 last month with two kids (6 and 9) and a very loud dog, sorry in advance. Happy to help with anything neighbourly, and we'd love to know where the good playground is.

**Short bio for a profile or panel:**
> Maya Chen is an engineer on [company]'s platform team, where she spent three years making payments fail less often. Before that she was at [previous]. She lives in Bristol and is slowly restoring a bike that will never be ridden.

**Meeting a client team for the first time:**
> I'm Nina, I'll be leading the design work on this. I've done similar rebuilds for [two names] and the thing I care most about is that it's still easy to use in a year. I'll be asking a lot of questions this week; none of them are traps.

## Avoid
- "Passionate about", "results-driven", "ninja".
- Listing every previous employer.
- Self-deprecation past one line.
- No invitation at the end (dead end).
- Long paragraphs.
- Jokes that need context.
