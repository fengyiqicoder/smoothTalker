# Sharing personal news

## Goal
The people who matter hear it from you, in the right order, with a signal for how to respond.

## Principles
- **Order matters.** Closest people first, individually. Then the group. People are hurt by hearing big news in a group chat or on social media before you told them.
- **Tell them how to respond.** "No need to reply" or "I'd love to hear from you" or "please don't ask about X yet" saves everyone guessing.
- **Short.** Big news does not need a long message. The news, one line of feeling, one line of what's next.
- **For bad news, decide what you want**: support, space, practical help, or just to have said it. Say which.
- **For good news, be mindful of the reader.** A pregnancy announcement to a friend struggling with fertility deserves an individual, gentle message. A new job to a friend who just lost theirs, likewise.

## Good news

**Engagement, individual to close friend:**
> Wanted you to hear it from me before it's anywhere else: we're engaged! It happened last night at the lake. I'm slightly in shock and very happy. Call you tomorrow with all of it.

**Engagement, group:**
> We're engaged! Happened Saturday, we're thrilled. No plans yet beyond a lot of cake. Thank you for being our people.

**Pregnancy, to a friend who's had a hard time with it:**
> I wanted to tell you this one directly and quietly, because I know it's not a simple thing for you: I'm pregnant, due in March. You don't have to say anything, and I don't need you to be anything but honest. I love you.

**New job:**
> Some news: I've accepted a role at [company], starting in June. It's a big step and I'm nervous in a good way. Thank you for listening to me agonise about it.

## Hard news

**Job loss, close friends:**
> Bit of news: I was laid off yesterday, along with about a third of the team. I'm okay, mostly, a bit winded. I'll be looking for something new from next week, so if you hear of anything, send it my way. Don't need anything else right now, just wanted you to know.

**Breakup, group of friends:**
> Sharing this once so I don't have to keep saying it: Sam and I have split up. It's sad and it's the right thing. I'm okay. I'd love company over the next few weeks; I'd rather not do a post-mortem on it. Thanks for being there.

**Diagnosis:**
> I want to tell you something and I'd rather do it in writing so I can get through it: I've been diagnosed with [X]. The plan is [one line]. I'm okay to talk about it, and I'll tell you what I need as I work it out. For now, just knowing you know helps. No need to reply with anything wise; "I'm here" is plenty.

**Moving away:**
> Some news I've been sitting on: I'm moving to [city] in September. It's for [reason] and it's the right move, but I'll miss you properly. Let's do a lot of things before then.

**Death in the family (letting people know):**
> I'm letting people know that my dad died on Tuesday. It was peaceful, with us there. The funeral is on the 18th at [place]; details to follow. I'm not up to much conversation right now but I'm glad you know.

## Responding to someone else's news
Good news: specific joy, one sentence, then a question. "Congratulations! I can hear how happy you are. When do you start?"
Hard news: acknowledge, don't fix. "I'm so sorry. Thank you for telling me. I'm here." See `condolences-and-support`.

## Avoid
- Announcing on social media before telling close people.
- Burying big news in the middle of a chatty message.
- Explaining the whole story in the announcement.
- "I know this is a lot" (they can decide).
- Apologising for having news.
