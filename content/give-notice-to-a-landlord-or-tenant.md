# Giving notice to a landlord or tenant

## Goal
The notice is valid, the dates are clear, and the handover goes smoothly enough that the deposit, the reference or the next tenancy is not put at risk. Get the facts right first, then make it kind.

Rent increases, repairs and getting a deposit back: `landlord-tenant-and-service-providers`.

## Structure
1. **Check the lease and local rules first.** Notice period, how notice must be served (email, letter, a specific form), and whether it must end on a rent date.
2. **State the notice plainly in the first sentence**, with the property address and the exact end date.
3. **Reference the lease clause or notice period** you are relying on.
4. **The practical plan:** final rent, viewings, key return, inspection, meter readings.
5. **Ask for written confirmation.**
6. **A warm close,** if the relationship allows it.

## Principles
- Put it in writing, in the form the lease requires, and keep a copy. A phone call or a text alone may not count as notice.
- Give the exact date, not "end of next month". Check that it meets the notice period.
- Rules differ a lot by country, state and tenancy type. When the lease is unclear or the stakes are high, check with a tenant advice service or a local housing body before sending.
- Asking to leave early is a request, not a notice. Make it easy to say yes: offer to help find a replacement tenant, cover advertising or pay rent until someone moves in.
- As a landlord, give more notice than the minimum when you can, the reason in one line, and practical help. A tenant who feels respected leaves the place in better shape.
- Do not use notice as a threat in a separate dispute.

## Examples

**Tenant, standard move-out notice (email or letter):**
> Dear Ms Harper, I'm writing to give notice that I will be leaving 14 Elm Road, Flat 2, with my tenancy ending on 30 June, in line with the one-month notice period in clause 8 of our lease. I'll pay June's rent as usual and return both sets of keys on the 30th. Could we arrange a check-out inspection that day? Please confirm you've received this notice. Thanks for two good years. Tom Reyes

**Tenant, asking to leave early:**
> Hi Mark, I've been offered a job in Leeds starting in August, which means I need to leave before the lease ends in December. Would you consider letting me end the tenancy on 31 July? I'm happy to pay rent until a new tenant moves in, help with viewings, and share the listing with people I know. I'd much rather sort this out together.

**Joint tenancy, one person leaving:**
> Hi Priya, Jess is moving out at the end of May, and Sam and I would like to stay. Can we add a new tenant to the lease in her place from 1 June? We have someone in mind and can send their details for your checks.

**Landlord, not renewing (selling the property):**
> Dear Chris and Ana, I'm writing to let you know that I've decided to sell the flat, so I won't be renewing your tenancy when it ends on 31 August. This is formal notice under the terms of your agreement. I know moving is a big disruption. I'll give at least 24 hours' notice for any viewings, and I'm very happy to write you a reference. Please let me know if you'd like to leave earlier, and I'll work with you on the dates.

**Landlord, confirming the handover plan:**
> Thanks for confirming the 30th. I'll meet you at 11am for the check-out, and we'll go through the inventory together and take meter readings. The deposit will be returned within the legal timeframe, minus any agreed deductions, and I'll send a written breakdown either way.

## Avoid
- Giving notice by text only, if the lease requires a letter or a form.
- Vague dates ("sometime next month").
- Threatening to withhold the last month's rent against the deposit, which many leases and laws do not allow.
- As a landlord, entering the property for viewings without the notice the law or lease requires.

## If it goes badly
If the landlord says your notice is invalid, ask them to point to the clause, check it against the lease and local rules, and if they are right, re-serve it correctly straight away rather than arguing. If they refuse an early exit, ask what would make it work (a replacement tenant, a fee) and get any agreement in writing. If a tenant does not leave by the end date, do not change locks or remove belongings; follow the legal process and get advice, since self-help evictions are illegal in most places.
> Thanks for flagging that. I've re-read clause 8 and you're right about the rent date, so please treat this as notice ending on 31 July instead.
> I understand you'd rather keep the lease as it is. Would you agree to release me if I find a replacement tenant who passes your checks? Happy to put that in writing.
