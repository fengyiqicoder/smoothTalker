# Ending a conversation

## Goal
The other person feels the conversation was complete, not abandoned, and you are free.

## Structure
1. **Signal the ending** with a summary or a warm recap line.
2. **Give a reason or none** (a reason is only needed if cutting off mid-flow).
3. **A forward hook** if you want to talk again; a clean close if you do not.
4. **Close.**

## Principles
- A conversation that trails off ("anyway...") makes the other person do the closing. Close it yourself.
- Do not fake a reason to leave. "I've got to run" when you do not is a small lie that people can sense. "I'm going to sign off" needs no reason.
- The last message should not be a question, unless you want it to continue.
- On text, the final message is often just an emoji or "talk soon". Do not overthink it.

## Examples

**Text thread that has run its course:**
> Okay, I'm going to head off. Really good to catch up, let's do it properly soon.

**Ending without a future promise:**
> Great talking. Take care of yourself.

**Phone call, someone who keeps talking:**
> I've got to jump off in a minute. Before I go, [one last thing]. Right, talk soon, bye.
Say "bye" and hang up. You do not need them to say it first.

**In person, party:**
> I'm going to go say hi to [person] before they leave. Really good to meet you.
Or: "I'm going to grab a drink, can I get you one?" (then do not come back).

**Group chat you want out of:**
> I'm going to duck out of this chat, my notifications are out of control. Message me directly any time.

**Someone messaging late at night:**
> Heading to bed. Talk tomorrow.

**A conversation that got heavy and you're tired:**
> I'm running out of steam and I don't want to give this less than it deserves. Can we pick it up tomorrow?

## Avoid
- "Anyway..." with nothing after it.
- Replying with shorter and shorter messages until they get the hint.
- Leaving on read after they asked something.
- "I'll let you go" (it is you who wants to go; own it).
- Multiple rounds of goodbye.
