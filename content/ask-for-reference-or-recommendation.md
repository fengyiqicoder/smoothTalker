# Asking for a reference or recommendation

## Goal
They say yes in one reply, know exactly what to say and by when, and feel good about being asked. The request is a ten-minute job for them, not a homework assignment.

## Structure
1. **Context in one line.** What you are applying for, or why you need the recommendation.
2. **The specific ask.** A phone reference, a written letter, a LinkedIn recommendation, a quote. Name it.
3. **Make it easy.** Three bullet points of what you would love them to mention, the deadline, and an offer of a draft they can edit.
4. **The exit.** "If the timing doesn't work, no problem at all." Give them a clean way out, and mean it.
5. **After they agree:** send the details in one place. Who will contact them, when, the job title, the bullets.

## Principles
- A reference request is a favour with a deadline. State the deadline in the first message, not after they agree.
- Bullets beat memory. They remember you were good; they do not remember the number you moved. Give them the number.
- Offer a draft for written recommendations. Most people accept and lightly edit it, and everyone's time is saved.
- Ask before you list them. Being called out of the blue about someone you have not spoken to in three years is a bad experience.
- After a long gap, lead with the reconnection and a reminder of what you worked on together. Do not pretend no time has passed.
- Do not stack asks. One reference, one deadline, one message.

## Examples

**Former manager, phone reference:**
> Hi Marta, I'm in the final round for a product lead role at Corvid and they've asked for two references. Would you be willing to be one? It'd be a 15 minute call sometime next week. If useful, the things they care most about are the Atlas launch, how I handled the vendor switch, and cross-team work. If the timing's bad, no problem at all.

**Professor, letter of recommendation:**
> Dear Professor Okafor, I'm applying to the MSc in Data Science at Edinburgh, deadline 15 January. Would you be able to write a letter of recommendation? I was in your Statistical Inference course in 2024 and did my final project on survival models for churn, which you gave an A. I'm happy to send a short draft, my CV and the submission link to make it quick. Thank you for considering it.

**LinkedIn recommendation, with a draft offered:**
> Hi Sam, I'm updating my LinkedIn and would value a short recommendation from you, since we worked together on the Hollis migration. Three or four sentences is plenty. If it helps, I can send a draft you can edit or throw out. No rush, any time in the next two weeks would be great.

**Client, case-study quote:**
> Hi Dana, we're putting together a short case study on the Q2 rollout and a line from you would carry a lot of weight. Something like: "[Company] cut our onboarding time from six days to two, and the team was easy to work with throughout." Feel free to edit, rewrite, or tell me it's not something you can do. Would you be okay with us using your name and title alongside it?

**Asking after a long gap:**
> Hi Tom, it's been a while, I hope things are good at Fenwick. I'm applying for a senior engineering role and thought of you, since you managed me through the platform rebuild in 2022. Would you be open to being a reference? Happy to catch you up over a quick call first so you're not going in cold.

**Sending the details after a yes:**
> Thank you, that means a lot. Here's everything in one place: the role is Senior Analyst at Corvid; Jess Lin (jess@corvid.com) will email you to arrange a call between the 8th and 12th; the points I mentioned were the Atlas launch, the vendor switch, and cross-team work. I'll let you know how it goes.

## Avoid
- "Could you maybe say a few words about me sometime?" with no role, no deadline, no format.
- Listing someone as a reference without asking.
- Sending the draft before they have said yes. Offer it, then send it.
- Chasing more than twice.
- Forgetting to close the loop. A one-line "I got the job, thank you" is the thing that makes them say yes next time.

## If they hesitate or decline

**They hesitate ("I'm not sure I'm the right person"):**
> That's fair. Would it help if I sent you a draft and the three points they'll ask about? If it still doesn't feel right, I'll ask someone else, no problem.

**They decline:**
> Understood, and thank you for telling me straight. I appreciate it.
Do not ask why. Ask someone else the same day. A reluctant reference is worse than a missing one.

**Silence after two messages:**
Treat it as a no. Move to your next name and, if you want the door open, send one line.
> No worries if this got buried. I've asked someone else for this one, but I'd still love to catch up sometime.
