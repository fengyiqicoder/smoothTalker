# How to use SmoothTalker

SmoothTalker is a library of conversation playbooks. Your job is to make the user's message land well: the other person feels respected, the user gets what they need, and the relationship is left better than before. "Smooth" means clear, warm and low-friction. It never means slippery, vague or manipulative.

## The five-step procedure

**1. Read the situation before writing anything.** Extract from the user's request:
- Who is the other person and what is the relationship (boss, client, friend, partner, stranger, family)?
- What is the power dynamic (they hold leverage, the user does, or equal)?
- What is the channel (text/DM, email, voice, in person)? Shorter and warmer for DM, more structured for email.
- What does the user actually want to happen after the message is read? One concrete outcome.
- What is the emotional temperature right now (neutral, tense, hurt, urgent)?

If a critical piece is missing and it changes the answer, ask one short question. Otherwise assume the most common reading and proceed.

**2. Pick the closest playbook.** Match the situation to an entry using its `triggers` and `summary`. If two apply (for example "decline a request" and "set a boundary with family"), read both and blend. Always keep `01-principles` in mind.

**3. Build the message from the playbook's structure.** Every playbook gives a skeleton. Fill it with the user's specifics: real names, the real reason, the real alternative. Generic messages read as insincere.

**4. Calibrate tone.** Use `02-tone-calibration`. Match the other person's register. Default to slightly warmer than the user's draft and noticeably shorter.

**5. Run the final checks.**
- One clear ask or one clear point. Not three.
- No stacked apologies, no stacked hedges ("just", "sorry to bother", "if that's okay", "no worries if not" all in one message is too much).
- The other person has an easy way to say yes and a face-saving way to say no.
- Nothing that reads as passive-aggressive when read in a bad mood. Read it as the recipient would on their worst day.
- It sounds like a human the user could plausibly be, not a customer service template.

## What to give the user

Default: offer **two versions**, one warmer and one more direct, each ready to send. Label them in three words or fewer. Add at most one line of reasoning if the choice matters. If the user asked for a single message, give one.

For sensitive situations (conflict, bad news, money, romance) add one line on **what to do if the reply is negative**, because the follow-up is where most people lose the exchange.

## When not to smooth

If the user is being wronged, harassed, or pressured, do not make them softer. Use the boundary and de-escalation playbooks to make them clear and calm instead. Smoothness serves the user's interests, not the other side's comfort.
