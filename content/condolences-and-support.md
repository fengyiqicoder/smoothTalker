# Condolences and support

## Goal
They feel less alone and are not handed any work: no need to reply, no need to manage your feelings, no need to decide what help to ask for.

## Structure
1. **Say the thing directly.** "I'm so sorry about your mum."
2. **One specific memory or quality**, if you knew the person or the situation. Specific is what people keep.
3. **One concrete offer**, or none. "I'm dropping food on your porch Thursday, no need to answer the door."
4. **Explicitly release them from replying.**

## Principles
- Short beats long. They are reading dozens of these.
- Do not explain the loss ("at least they're not suffering", "everything happens for a reason", "they're in a better place"). Explaining is for you.
- Do not ask "how are you?" as an opener. The answer is obvious and it forces performance.
- "Let me know if you need anything" transfers the work to them. Offer something specific, or just be present.
- Say the person's name. People fear their loved one being forgotten more than they fear being reminded.

## Examples

**Death of a parent, text:**
> I'm so sorry about your dad. I keep thinking about him teaching us all to skim stones that summer. I'm here for whatever you need, and I'll check in next week. You don't need to reply to this.

**Illness diagnosis:**
> I heard about the diagnosis. I'm not going to pretend I know the right thing to say. I love you and I'm around, and I'm going to keep being around. I'll text every few days; reply or don't, both are fine.

**Breakup:**
> Heard about you and Dan. I'm sorry, that's a lot. Want company or want space? Either's fine and you can change your mind hourly.

**Miscarriage (they told you directly):**
> I'm so sorry. I'm not going to say anything except that I'm here, and I'll keep being here, and you don't have to be okay.

**Colleague, peer (you don't control their workload):**
> Raj, I'm so sorry about your dad. Don't give work a second thought; whatever lands on my side I'll cover. Thinking of you and your family, no need to reply.

**Manager to report, card or email:**
> I was very sorry to hear about the loss of your mother. Please don't think about work at all right now; we've got everything covered. Take whatever time you need. Thinking of you and your family.

**Weeks or months later (this matters more than the first message):**
> Thinking about you today. No need to reply.

## Avoid
- "Let me know if there's anything I can do."
- "At least..."
- "I know exactly how you feel."
- Comparing to your own loss in the first message.
- Silence because you did not know what to say. A short imperfect message beats no message.

## Follow-up cadence
Day 1, then a week, then a month, then the anniversary. Most people disappear after the funeral; being the one who does not is the whole thing.
