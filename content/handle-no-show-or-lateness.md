# Handling a no-show or lateness

## Goal
The plan gets rescued or rescheduled, the other person knows what you noticed, and nobody has to pretend it did not happen. Warm on the person, clear on the pattern.

## They are running late or did not show (one-off)
1. **Check they are okay.** One line, no sarcasm.
2. **State what you will do.** "I'll wait till 7:30" or "I'm heading off, let's rebook."
3. **Give them a concrete next step** if you still want to meet.

## You were late or did not show
1. **Name it specifically.** "I missed our 6pm" not "sorry about earlier".
2. **Own it without a "but".** Context, if needed, is one clause after the apology.
3. **Acknowledge their cost.** Their time, their evening, the table they held.
4. **Propose a fix with a date.** Do not ask them to do the rescheduling work.
5. **One make-good if it fits.** "Coffee's on me."

## Friend who cancels last minute, repeatedly
1. **Say you enjoy seeing them.** Real, not a softener.
2. **Name the pattern with numbers.** "Three of the last four."
3. **Say the effect on you, one line.** "I stop planning around it."
4. **Ask what would actually work.** Shorter, closer, more spontaneous. Let them tell you.
5. **No ultimatum.** You are adjusting how you plan, not threatening the friendship.

## Professional no-show (client, vendor, contractor)
1. **Factual and dated.** "We had 10am today; I waited until 10:30."
2. **Consequence, if there is one.** Fee, missed slot, delayed timeline. Stated, not threatened.
3. **One rebooking option** with a specific time.
4. **What happens if it recurs**, said once, calmly.

## Principles
- Sarcasm feels good for a minute and costs you the conversation.
- Numbers beat adjectives. "Always" gets argued; "three of the last four" does not.
- Say what you will do, not what they must do. That is the difference between a boundary and a demand.
- If you are the one who flaked, the apology is short and the fix is specific. Long apologies make them comfort you.
- A professional no-show is a business matter. Keep it in business language and put it in writing.

## Examples

**They're 20 minutes late, no word:**
> Hey, all okay? I'm at the table by the window. I'll hang on till 7:30, after that let's do another night.

**They no-showed, one-off:**
> Missed you tonight, hope nothing's wrong. I'm free Thursday or Sunday if you want to try again, your pick.

**You're running late:**
> Running 15 minutes behind, my fault, left too late. Order without me and I'll catch up. There by 7:15.

**You no-showed a friend:**
> I completely missed our lunch today and I'm sorry. No excuse, I had the wrong day in my head. You held a table and gave up your break for it. Can I take you Friday, same place, my treat?

**Friend who cancels last minute, naming the pattern:**
> I love hanging out with you, and the last three plans have all been cancelled the day of. I'm not upset, I just want to find something that works. Would shorter and closer suit better, like a coffee near yours on a weekday? Or tell me what would.

**Friend who is always late, before the next meet-up:**
> Booking 7 for Friday. Fair warning, I'll order at 7:15 whether you're there or not, so I'm not sitting with an empty plate. See you then.

**Client no-show, email:**
> Hi [name], we had a call scheduled for 10am today and I waited until 10:30. No problem if something came up. I can offer Thursday at 2pm or Friday at 11am. Please note that a second missed session without 24 hours' notice is charged at the full rate, as per our agreement.

**Vendor or contractor no-show:**
> Hi [name], you were booked for 9am today and nobody arrived or called. I've taken the morning off for this. Can you confirm a new date by end of tomorrow? If I don't hear back I'll need to book someone else.

## Avoid
- "No worries!" when there are worries. It teaches them the flaking is free.
- "Typical" or "as usual". You lose the moral high ground and the point.
- Asking "why?" They will give a reason and you will have to accept it.
- An ultimatum with a friend. "If you cancel again we're done" makes the next cancellation a crisis.
- Over-apologising when you were late by five minutes. Match the size.
- Waiting silently for an hour then exploding. Set the limit early and out loud.

## If it goes badly
If they get defensive about the pattern:
> I'm not keeping score to punish you. I'd rather say it than quietly stop inviting you. Let's try the coffee idea and see.

If a friend keeps cancelling after you named it, stop making fixed plans and say so, once, without heat:
> No stress at all. I'm going to stop booking things in advance for now, but if you're ever free last minute, text me and I'll come if I can.

If a client argues the no-show fee:
> I understand it's frustrating. The policy was in the agreement you signed, and I apply it to everyone. Thursday at 2 is still open if you'd like it.
