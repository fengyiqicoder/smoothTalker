# Cold outreach

## Goal
A reply. Not a sale, not a meeting; a reply. Everything is optimised for making the reply cheap for them.

## Structure (four lines, under 100 words)
1. **Why them, specifically.** One line proving this is not a blast. Reference their work, a recent post, a problem they have publicly.
2. **What you have**, in one plain sentence. No jargon.
3. **Proof**, one number or one name.
4. **Tiny ask.** A yes/no question, or "worth a look?" Not "hop on a 30-minute call".

## Principles
- Subject line: specific and short. "Idea for [their product]'s onboarding" beats "Partnership opportunity".
- No "I hope this finds you well." No "my name is X and I'm the founder of Y" as the opener.
- Write it for a phone screen. If the ask is not visible without scrolling, it is too long.
- Give them a way to say no that is not silence: "If it's not relevant, a 'no' is genuinely helpful."
- Follow up twice, spaced a week apart, each with a new piece of value. Then stop.
- One ask. Not "call, or demo, or intro to your team".

## Examples

**Sales, email:**
> Subject: 40% of your checkout drop-off
> Hi Lena,
> I noticed [brand]'s checkout asks for an account before payment; that's typically where a third of mobile buyers leave. We built a guest checkout layer that [similar brand] used to lift conversion 18% in six weeks. Worth a 10-minute look? If not relevant, a "no" is genuinely useful.
> Sam

**Partnership, DM:**
> Hi Priya, your newsletter's piece on solo founders' burnout was the most shared thing in our community last month. We run a 12k-member group for exactly that audience. I have one specific idea for a joint piece that'd be easy on your side. Open to hearing it in two lines?

**Press / journalist:**
> Hi Marcus, you covered the rise of agentic shopping in June and asked what happens when Amazon opts out. We just published data from 2,000 shoppers on exactly that; the headline is [one stat]. Happy to send the full dataset, no strings. Useful?

**Asking for an intro (via mutual contact):**
> Hi Jo, could I ask a small favour? I'd love an intro to Dana at [company]; I think our onboarding tool solves the churn issue she posted about last week. I've drafted a two-line blurb you could forward so it's zero work for you. Totally fine if it's not appropriate.

**Follow-up 1 (a week later):**
> Bumping this with one more thing: we just published [resource] that's relevant to what you're doing. No pressure on the original ask.

**Follow-up 2 (final):**
> Last note from me. If the timing's off, no worries at all; I'll leave it here. Door's open if it becomes useful.

## Avoid
- Long intros about yourself.
- Multiple links.
- "Let me know if you have any questions."
- "I'd love to pick your brain."
- Asking for 30 minutes.
- Flattery without specifics.
- Sending the same message to ten people at one company.
