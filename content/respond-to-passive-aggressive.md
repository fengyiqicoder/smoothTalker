# Responding to passive-aggression

## Goal
You do not escalate, you do not grovel, and you give them an off-ramp to say what they actually mean.

## The three options
1. **Take it at face value.** Respond to the literal content, warmly, as if there were no dig. This defuses most cases because it denies the dig any oxygen.
2. **Name it gently and ask.** "I might be reading tone into this, but are you annoyed about something? Genuinely asking."
3. **Move to a richer channel.** "Can I call you? Text is making this weirder than it needs to be."

Use 1 for one-off snark. Use 2 when it is a pattern or clearly about something real. Use 3 for people you care about.

## Principles
- Do not match the tone. Matching is what they are (often unconsciously) inviting.
- Do not interpret the dig out loud ("so what you're saying is I'm useless"). You will be told you are overreacting.
- Short and warm beats long and defensive.
- If they are angry about something legitimate, the passive-aggression is the wrapper; deal with the contents.
- Silence is a legitimate response to a pure dig with no content.

## Examples

**"Must be nice to have time for the gym."**
Face value:
> It really is! I've been forcing myself. Want to come Thursday?

**"Fine." / "Whatever you want."**
Name it:
> That sounds like not-fine. What would you actually prefer? I'd rather know.

**"Thanks for finally replying."**
Face value with a small own:
> Sorry it took me a while, this week's been a lot. What's up?

**"I guess I'll just do it myself then."**
Name and offer:
> Sounds like you're frustrated I didn't get to it. Fair. Do you want me to take it now, or has that ship sailed?

**A sarcastic message in a group chat aimed at you:**
Face value, briefly, then move on:
> Ha, fair. Anyway, Saturday: who's in?

**Cold email from a colleague ("Per my last email...")**
Warm and factual:
> Thanks for resending, I'd missed it. Answers below. Let me know if anything's still unclear.

**Repeated pattern with someone close:**
> I've noticed a few messages lately that feel a bit sharp and I don't want to guess at what's going on. If something's bothering you, I'd honestly rather hear it straight. If I'm imagining it, tell me that too.

## Avoid
- "Wow, okay."
- Sarcasm back.
- A paragraph explaining yourself.
- "What's that supposed to mean?" (invites a fight; use "are you annoyed?" instead).
- Screenshotting it to others before talking to them.
