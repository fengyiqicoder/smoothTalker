# Delivering bad news

## Goal
They understand the news in the first two lines, feel it was delivered with care, and know what happens next.

## Structure
1. **One line of warning** so they brace: "I've got some news you won't love."
2. **The news, plainly, early.** Do not make them read three paragraphs to find it.
3. **One sentence of why**, if there is a why they need. Not a defence.
4. **Acknowledge what it means for them.**
5. **What happens next / what you can offer.**
6. **Availability**: "I'm around if you want to talk."

## Principles
- Delay is cruelty. The kindest version is the fast one.
- Do not soften the news into ambiguity. "It might be tricky" when you mean "no" makes them hope and then hurts twice.
- Do not make them comfort you. Your feelings about delivering it are for someone else.
- Deliver in the richest channel you reasonably can. Text for small things, call for big ones. If it has to be text, say you are happy to talk.

## Examples

**Plans fell through, text:**
> Bad news: the cabin fell through, the owner double-booked. I'm really sorry, I know you'd cleared the weekend. I've found two other options nearby and I'll send them tonight. Still on if you are.

**You can't pay someone back on time:**
> I need to be straight with you. I can't pay you the £400 on Friday like I said. I can do £200 Friday and the rest on the 28th. I'm sorry for the change, I know you were counting on it.

**Telling a friend you're not coming to their wedding abroad:**
> I've gone back and forth on this and I don't want to keep you guessing: I'm not going to be able to make it to Portugal. It's the cost, honestly, and I'd rather tell you now than keep you hoping. I'm gutted. I'd love to do something to celebrate you both here before or after.

**Telling someone a mutual friend is unwell, message:**
> I wanted you to hear this from me rather than through the grapevine. Tom's been diagnosed with [X]. He's okay to talk about it and asked me to let people close to him know. Call me if you want to talk it through.

**Email, project cancelled:**
> Hi team,
> I'll get straight to it: the Harbour project has been cancelled by the client as of this morning. This isn't about the quality of the work, which they were explicit about. I know several of you have put months into it and that's a hard thing to hear. I'll be meeting each of you this week to talk about what you move to next. My door's open before then.
> Sam

## Avoid
- "I don't know how to say this..." followed by not saying it.
- Burying the news after praise.
- "Everything happens for a reason."
- Listing all the ways it is not your fault.

## If they react badly
> I get it. Take the time you need. I'm not going anywhere.
Do not argue with the reaction.
