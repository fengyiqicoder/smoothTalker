# Apologising publicly as a brand or small business

## Goal
Affected customers feel told the truth and taken care of, and everyone else sees a business that owns its mistakes. A public apology is mostly information. "Sorry" appears once, early; the rest is what you did, what you are doing and what changes.

## Structure
1. **What happened, plainly, first sentence.** "Our site was down for six hours on Tuesday."
2. **Who was affected and how.** Numbers if you have them.
3. **Sorry, once, from a named person.**
4. **What you have already done.**
5. **What affected customers get and how**, automatic where possible.
6. **What changes so it does not repeat.** One or two specifics, not a "full review".
7. **How to reach a human.**

## Principles
- Speed over polish. A short honest post within hours beats a lawyered statement in three days.
- Match the size. A wrong price needs three sentences. A data incident needs a full email and a pinned post.
- Promise only what you can verify. "Never again" is unkeepable. "A second check before prices go live" is not.
- Do not name or blame an employee publicly. Own it as the business.
- For a data incident, check your legal obligations on notification timing and content in every region where you have customers before you post, and say plainly what data was and was not involved.

## Examples

**Outage, social post:**
> Our checkout was down from 9am to 3pm today and about 1,200 of you couldn't place orders. Sorry, that's on us: a failed update, now rolled back. Anything ordered today ships free, code TODAY until Sunday. Charged without a confirmation? Email me at [address] and I'll sort it within the day. Jo, founder.

**Bad batch, email to buyers:**
> Subject: Your order from batch 0417 and what we're doing
> Hi [first name],
> The hot sauce you ordered between 2 and 9 April came from a batch that didn't ferment properly. It's safe, but it tastes flat and some bottles separate. I'm sorry. A replacement ships this week at no cost, no action needed. If you'd rather have a refund, reply "refund" and it's done. We now test every batch before bottling rather than every third one.
> Kemi

**Rude employee, reply to a public complaint:**
> I've read your post and I'm sorry that's how you were spoken to on Saturday. That's not the standard here and I've dealt with it. I'm sending you a DM now to refund your visit and invite you back as my guest. Thank you for telling us rather than just not coming back. Ana, owner.

**Pricing mistake, post:**
> For about 18 hours yesterday our site showed the leather tote at $19 instead of $190. We've honoured all 340 orders placed at that price; they ship next week. Sorry to anyone who saw it change mid-checkout. A second sign-off now sits before any price goes live.

**Data incident, email (check your legal obligations first):**
> Subject: A security incident affecting your account
> Hi [first name],
> On 12 June we found that an unauthorised person accessed a database containing customer names, email addresses and order history between 3 and 11 June. It did not contain passwords or payment details, which are stored separately and were not accessed. I'm sorry. We closed the access within two hours, reset all internal credentials and brought in an outside security firm. Be cautious of emails claiming to be from us that ask for payment or login details; we never ask for those by email. Questions: reply here or call [number]. We'll update you by [date].
> Marco, CEO

**Social media misstep, pinned post:**
> Yesterday's post was thoughtless and it hurt people. It's down. Sorry, no excuses: it shouldn't have been written or approved. From now on two people sign off on every post. Comments are open and we're reading.

**Pinned comment under the apology:**
> Affected and not heard from us by Thursday? Email [address] with your order number and I'll reply personally. Jo.

## Replying to angry comments under the apology
Silence under your own apology looks like you posted and ran. Reply to each specific comment briefly and move detail to DM: "You're right, and I'm sorry. Sending you a DM now to fix your order." If a comment is wrong on the facts, correct once, calmly: "The outage was six hours, not two days. Anyone who ordered that day gets free shipping, including you." If a commenter is abusive, one reply, then stop. Do not delete unless it breaks the platform's rules or yours.

## Avoid
- "We apologise for any inconvenience."
- "Mistakes were made."
- "We take this very seriously."
- "Some customers may have experienced."
- Blaming a vendor, a platform or a junior employee in public.
- Promising a "full internal review" with no specifics.
- Posting and going silent.
- Deleting critical comments.

## If it goes badly
If the apology gets more heat than the original problem, do not apologise for the apology. Reply to comments with actions taken and let the fixes speak. If someone says "words are cheap", agree: "Fair. Here's what's done so far: [two actions]. I'll post an update on [date]." Then post it on that date.
