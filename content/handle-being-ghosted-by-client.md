# Client or prospect went silent

## Goal
Either they re-engage because you made it easy, or you close the file cleanly and stop spending energy on it. Both are wins.

## Principles
- Silence after a proposal usually means: priorities shifted, budget got cut, they're embarrassed to say no, or your email is buried. Almost never personal.
- Each follow-up should make replying easier than the last: fewer options, smaller ask.
- Offer the no explicitly. People ghost because they hate saying no; give them a one-word way to do it.
- Three touches, then a clean close. The close often gets a reply.
- Add value in at least one follow-up. New information gives them a reason to reply that is not "sorry I ignored you".

## The sequence

**Touch 1 (one week after silence): assume buried.**
> Hi Ravi, bumping the proposal from the 3rd in case it got lost. Happy to adjust anything. Would it help to have a 15-minute call to go through it, or would you rather I just answer questions by email?

**Touch 2 (two weeks later): add value, shrink the ask.**
> Hi Ravi, since I sent the proposal we've finished a similar project for [company] and I thought the results might be useful context: [one line]. Is this still something you're looking at this quarter? A quick yes / no / not yet is all I need.

**Touch 3 (two weeks later): the clean close.**
> Hi Ravi, I'll assume the timing isn't right and close this on my side so I'm not cluttering your inbox. If it comes back up, I'd genuinely like to help; just reply to this thread. Best of luck with [their project].

## Variations

**Existing client who's gone quiet mid-project:**
> Hi Ravi, I haven't heard back on the draft from the 10th and I want to make sure we're still on track. If there's a hold-up on your side, no problem, just let me know so I can plan my schedule. If you'd like me to pause, say the word.

**When you need a decision by a date:**
> I'm holding time for this project in October, and I need to release it to someone else if we're not proceeding. Could you let me know by Friday either way?

**Reply to a "sorry for the silence":**
> No apology needed, I know how it goes. Where would you like to pick up from?

## Avoid
- "Just checking in!" with nothing else.
- "I've tried reaching you several times..."
- Guilt-tripping.
- Following up daily.
- A long re-pitch.
- Leaving it open forever. Close it, it's healthier and often gets a reply.
