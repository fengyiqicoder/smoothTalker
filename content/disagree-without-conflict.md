# Disagreeing without conflict

## Goal
Your view is on the table, clearly, and the other person does not feel diminished for holding theirs.

## Structure
1. **Reflect the part of their view you can honestly agree with**, or the goal you share.
2. **Signal the disagreement** ("here's where I land differently").
3. **Your view, stated as your view**, with one reason.
4. **Curiosity**: a genuine question about their reasoning, or an invitation to correct you.

## Principles
- "And" not "but" after the acknowledgement. "But" erases what came before.
- Disagree with the idea, not the person. "I see it differently" not "you're wrong."
- One reason, your strongest. Three reasons sound like a case for the prosecution.
- Offer your confidence level honestly. "I'm fairly sure" and "I could be wrong here" are both useful, if true.
- In a group, disagree in a way that lets them save face in front of others. Or take it private.

## Examples

**Friend's opinion, casual:**
> I actually see that film differently. I found the ending rushed, mostly because we never saw why she changed her mind. What made it work for you?

**Group chat, someone says something you think is off:**
> I'd gently push back on that one. I don't think the new bike lanes caused the traffic, the roadworks on Hill Street started the same week. Could be missing something though.

**Partner, about a decision:**
> I hear that you want to drive through the night, and I know it's the faster option. I'm not comfortable with it because we'd both be exhausted on the motorway. Could we stop halfway and leave early instead?

**Someone senior, in a meeting:**
> I want to make sure I understand the goal. If it's hitting the March launch, I'd suggest cutting the reporting feature rather than adding weekend work, because it's the piece with the most open bugs. Happy to be wrong on this.

**Disagreeing with a plan everyone likes:**
> I'm going to be the annoying one. I'm worried the venue has no step-free access, and two of the guests use wheelchairs. If we've already thought about it and I missed it, ignore me.

## Avoid
- "With all due respect."
- "Actually,"
- "I'm just playing devil's advocate."
- Repeating your point louder when they do not agree the first time.
- Piling on evidence. If one strong reason did not move them, five will not either.
- Winning. Being right and losing the relationship is not smooth.

## If it escalates
If they get heated, stop adding reasons and lower the stakes: name that you disagree, that it is fine to, and step off. If the disagreement matters for a decision (work, money, a shared plan), move from who is right to what you will do: agree a test, a trial period, or who gets the final call. If they keep returning to it, you do not have to keep defending your view; saying it once was enough.
> I don't think we're going to agree on this one, and that's okay. I'd rather keep this easy between us than win the point.
> We see it differently, and it's your call on this one. I've said my piece, I'll back whatever you decide.
