# Out of office and away messages

## Goal
The sender knows in ten seconds whether to wait, go elsewhere, or escalate, and they do not feel brushed off. You get to be away without a pile of "did you see this?" follow-ups.

## Structure
1. **Dates.** When you left, when you are back, in the sender's terms ("back Monday 12 October").
2. **What happens to their message.** Read on return, not read, or forwarded.
3. **Who to contact instead**, with one route each: urgent versus everything else. Name a person, not "the team". Working solo with nobody covering? Say so, then either give one narrow emergency route you will actually check ("text [number] if a live project breaks, I check once mid-week") or say plainly that everything waits.
4. **One line of warmth or context** if it fits you. Optional.
5. **Nothing else.** No apology, no list of what you are doing on holiday.

## Principles
- Under 60 words for most. Auto-replies get skimmed on phones.
- Be honest about whether you will read it. "I'll reply when I'm back" is a promise; only make it if you will.
- Give the cover person a heads-up before you name them, and put their name in exactly one place.
- Humour works when it is one line, self-directed, and does not delay the information. It fails when it is the whole message, mocks the sender, or forces them to hunt for the dates.
- For sick leave and bereavement, give less detail, not more. "Away for a few weeks" is enough. Your colleagues can say more if they need to.
- Small businesses: put the reopening date and how to order in the meantime. Customers mostly want to know if they can still buy.
- Freelancers at capacity: an "away" reply is still a sales message. Give the next available date and make it easy to book it.

## Examples

**Vacation, standard:**
> I'm away until Monday 12 October and not reading email. For anything urgent, Nadia Weiss (nadia@[company]) is covering. Everything else, I'll reply in the week I'm back.

**Vacation, with humour that does not backfire:**
> I'm out until 12 October, somewhere with patchy signal and excellent bread. Nadia (nadia@[company]) has my projects until then; I'll reply to everything else when I'm back.

**Parental leave:**
> I'm on parental leave until early February and won't be checking this inbox. For [product] questions, please contact Jordan Reyes (jordan@[company]). For everything else, hello@[company] will route you. Thanks for your patience.

**Sick leave (keep it minimal):**
> I'm on leave for a few weeks and not reading email. Please contact Amir Haddad (amir@[company]) for anything active. Messages here won't be forwarded, so do resend if it's important.

**Sabbatical:**
> I'm on sabbatical until March 2027 and this inbox is closed. Client matters: Priya Nair (priya@[company]). Speaking or press: office@[company]. If it can wait until spring, I'd love to hear from you then.

**Small business closed for a holiday (email):**
> We're closed for the holidays from 23 December, reopening Monday 5 January. Online orders still go through and ship from the 5th. Anything ordered before the 20th has already shipped. Thanks for a great year.

**WhatsApp or Instagram away status for a shop:**
> Closed for the holidays, back Tue 6 Jan. Order online any time at [link]; we'll reply to messages when we reopen.

**Freelancer at capacity:**
> Thanks for getting in touch. I'm fully booked until mid-November, so I can't take new work before then. If your timeline allows, reply with a line about the project and I'll hold a slot for the week of 17 November. If you need someone sooner, I'm happy to suggest a name.

## Avoid
- "I have limited access to email." Either you read it or you do not.
- Listing everyone in the department with a topic each.
- "Sorry for any inconvenience." Being away is not a fault.
- Jokes about how much you hate work, or about the sender's timing.
- Leaving the auto-reply on after you return.
- Detailed medical or family information in a sick-leave reply.
- Forgetting the date format: "back on the 5th" means nothing on the 27th of another month.

## When you are back
Send this to a customer who wrote during the closure, before they have to chase:
> Hi Elena, thanks for your message on the 28th, and thanks for waiting while we were closed. I've checked and the order is in stock; it'll ship today and you'll have tracking by this evening. If anything else came up in the meantime, I'm here.
If you cannot fix it immediately:
> Thanks for your patience over the break. I'm back today and I'm looking at this first thing; you'll have a proper answer from me by tomorrow noon.
If someone is annoyed they had to wait:
> That's fair, a week is a long time to wait for a reply. I'm on it now and I'll have it sorted by [time]. Thanks for sticking with us.
